# Finish-Line Crumbs Are the Worker's Only Edge

Nowhere in the territory surveyed does a 45-second, $1–$25 taker on Polymarket US hold a dependable edge, with one narrow exception: buying the already-decided side of a sports moneyline at **0.97–0.99 in the minutes around the final whistle**, which on the venue's own minute tape pays **0.94–2.88% per cycle after fee** against a measured post-finish surprise rate of **4 in 9,273 games (0.04%)**, at least two of them tape artefacts, versus a break-even loss rate of 0.93–2.80%; that trade is capped at roughly **$1–7 a day gross** on a $135 account and needs a score signal and a depth read the worker does not yet have. The low-attention hypothesis fails on the evidence: ITF, Setka Cup, esports and top-flight overseas soccer books are **one cent wide, carry about 10^5 resting shares a side, and repriced in 20 of 28 five-second polls**, the signature of automated professional makers quoting off an odds feed, and buying the in-play favourite there earns **−2 to +1 points against cost**, the same as in the four US majors; the books that really are neglected (darts, Czech Liga Pro, TT Elite, AHL, KHL) are neglected by being **8–32 cents wide**. Non-sport listings are 81% midterm contracts whose liquid legs sit within a tick of Kalshi and global Polymarket, the thin threshold ladders leak only occasional stale bids worth single-digit dollars, and the BTC 15-minute "certainty" trade cannot be executed because the winning side has **no sellers at all in the final 20–180 seconds**. Cross-venue lag runs the wrong way: Polymarket US moves with Kalshi inside the same minute (change correlation 0.96–0.99 in play) and **leads global Polymarket by a median 4–6 minutes** in live games, so reading other venues gives the worker a stale signal, not a fresh one. Market making earns no rebate below $10M of monthly maker volume, Dutch books were **zero in 13,909 two-sided markets**, and the worker's own data path has a hidden cost: the public gateway is edge-cached for 30 seconds, so a 45-second poll reads an ask that is 0–75 seconds old. The ranked recommendation is to build the settlement-yield rule small and instrumented, bolt on an opportunistic ladder stale-bid check, and do nothing with everything else.

## A 30-second edge cache leaves the poller reading asks up to 75 seconds old

The fee model the worker already uses is correct and venue-wide, but one assumption underneath it is wrong. The official schedule effective 1:00 PM ET on 19 October 2026 is **Fee = 0.0695 × C × p × (1 − p)** for takers, **0.10 for table tennis**, with maker rebates starting only at $10M of prior-month maker volume and taker rebates only at $250K of prior-month taker volume ([Fee Schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)); a scan of all 22,192 open markets on 24 October found `feeCoefficient` = 0.0695 on every one ([gateway events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?closed=false&limit=500&offset=0)). For a $135 account that means maker fee zero, maker rebate zero and the full taker curve: 0.70% of outlay at 0.90, 3.5% at 0.50. The coefficient has been raised three times in 2026 (0.05 → 0.06 on 1 July, → 0.0695 on 17 September, table tennis → 0.10 on 7 October) by weekly notice ([CFTC filing 09212628660](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09212628660.pdf)), so the worker should read `feeCoefficient` per market rather than hard-code it; a Setka Cup trade modelled at 0.0695 understates its cost by 44%.

The wrong assumption is freshness. On 24 October `GET /v1/markets/{slug}/bbo` on the public gateway returned `Cache-Control: public, max-age=30` with `CF-Cache-Status: HIT` and an `Age` header climbing from 3 to 29 across eleven calls while the embedded `lastPriceSample.ts` stayed fixed; the twelfth call was `EXPIRED` and the sample advanced ([gateway BBO](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/aec-boxing-canalv-chrmbi-2026-10-31-canalv-chrmbi/bbo)). Stacked on a 45-second poll, the worker's view of the ask lags the matching engine by **0 to 75 seconds**, which by itself explains part of the adverse selection the earlier favorites and standing-order backtests found. The cure is cheap: the authenticated WebSocket at `wss://api.polymarket.us/v1/ws/markets` offers full-book, lite-BBO and trade streams for up to 100 markets per subscription using the same Ed25519 key, and the docs themselves recommend it over polling `/bbo` ([Markets WebSocket](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/websocket/markets.md); [Rate Limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rate-limits.md)). The REST budget is 25 requests per second per source IP, shared across every process on that IP, and in practice three concurrent pollers drew HTTP 429 on 10 of 118 requests at a combined rate near 10 per second ([Rate Limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rate-limits.md)). The API also supports the extensions the user named: `participateDontInitiate` for true post-only resting orders, FOK to avoid partial fills on a thin touch, DAY/GTC/GTD time in force, batched create/cancel/modify, and a `/book` depth endpoint ([Create Order](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/orders/create-order.md)).

Three further mechanics shape every strategy below. First, the venue pays nothing on idle cash or held positions; the Participant Agreement's only "interest" is the security interest pledged to the clearinghouse, and collateral is 100% of the at-risk amount with no release on favourable moves ([Participant Agreement](https://polymarketexchange.com/files/legal/latest/participant-agreement); [Collateral and Margin](https://docs.polymarket.us/market-structure/collateral-and-margin.md)). Second, sports contracts settle "often shortly after the official result is posted" with no published latency, ties at $0.50, and cancellations or no-contests at last fair market price rather than $1 ([Sports FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/sports-faqs.md)); the venue's recorded `finishedTimestamp` lags the end of two-sided trading by a **median 2.7 minutes and a 90th percentile of 59 minutes** on the cached tapes, so it cannot serve as a real-time "final" signal ([gateway price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history)). Third, the Rulebook treats bots as expected but regulated: Rule 5.2(i) requires "order throttles, price collars, and kill switches" for automated systems, 5.2(h) an electronic audit trail, 7.2(q) bans self-matching across commonly controlled accounts "whether Orders are entered directly, via API, or through an FCM," and §19 of the Participant Agreement conditions live API use on written approval ([Rulebook, March 2026](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules03202641475.pdf)). Nothing in any of these documents prohibits reading other venues' prices or taking stale quotes. All open orders are cancelled before the Thursday 6:00–8:00 AM ET maintenance window and books reopen empty ([General FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/general-faqs.md)), which matters for any resting-order design.

## Minor leagues reprice every five to ten seconds, not more slowly

The user's first question was whether overseas and lower-attention leagues update more slowly. They do not; they are, if anything, the fastest books on the venue. The researcher replayed about **6,200 settled games across 45 league slugs** (ATP/WTA, ATP qualifying, ITF M15–W75, UTR, four Setka Cup slugs, Czech Liga Pro, TT Elite, TT Cup, WTT, CS2, LoL, Dota 2, Valorant, EPL through the English National League, MLB/NPB, AHL/KHL, T20I/IPL cricket, darts, boxing/UFC and BETER's eBattles) through the repository's favorites backtest on the venue's one-minute price history, yielding 48,110 paper positions, and supplemented it with a 145-second, five-second-interval live poll of eight in-play matches ([gateway events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?closed=false&limit=500&offset=0); tables in `research_notes/Polymarket US worker edge survey/low_attention_sports_data/analysis_tables.txt`). The live poll is the decisive picture: an ITF M15 first-set match showed **bid 0.55 / ask 0.56 with 110,000 shares on each side over 41–43 levels**, and the top of book changed in 20 of 28 polls; an ITF women's match quoted 0.29/0.30 with 130,000 shares a side and changed 16 of 28 times; three Setka Cup Ukraine matches in deciding sets carried 73,000–91,000 shares per side and repriced in 18–19 of 28 polls. Median in-play spreads from the tape were 1¢ on ATP, WTA, ATP qualifying, ITF men's/women's, UTR, Setka Ukraine and Czechia, CS2, LoL, EPL, La Liga and UCL, with 68–99% of in-play minutes at or under 4¢.

That is not retail. It is consistent with the reported arrangement under which **Jump Trading receives Polymarket equity that "scales based on the liquidity the firm provides"** to the US operation ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357519?amp=1)), with the in-house market-making desk Polymarket was recruiting ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-in-house-market-making-desk-trade-users)), and with the venue's Series-Based Market Maker Program ([CFTC filing 09302630733](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09302630733.pdf)). The liquidity-reward pools rule out the alternative explanation that someone quotes ITF for the rewards: "ITF ML Live" paid **$52 across 338 markets**, "Table Tennis Moneyline Live" $17 across 317, "Ebattles Moneyline Live" $6 across 525, against $9,500 for "NFL Spreads Live" ([polymarket.us/rewards](https://polymarket.us/rewards)). Makers are on these books because they can price them from a feed at low risk, which is the opposite of an unmaintained book. The genuinely unmaintained books exist, but in the other direction: median in-play spreads of **15¢ on Czech Liga Pro, 32¢ on PDC darts, 18¢ on TT Elite, 9–10¢ on TT Cup and WTT, 8¢ on AHL and 13¢ on KHL**, with a tight (≤4¢) book in 0–13% of minutes. A taker crossing those pays the whole width; there is no stale one-cent book anywhere in the sample.

The backtest numbers confirm what the microstructure predicts. Buying the in-play favourite on a tight book, with the market's own fee, gave the following edge in points (win rate minus mean cost, ± game-clustered standard error, n positions):

Every thick low-tier market sits within one standard error of zero, and the fee (0.9¢ at 85¢, 0.3¢ at 95¢ at Θ = 0.0695; 1.3¢ and 0.5¢ on Setka at 0.10) is roughly the size of the whole deviation. The two positive cells are cricket and the EFL Championship, and both are too small to separate from luck: **71 of 71 wins at 0.95 has a one-sided 95% lower bound of about 95.8%, which equals the 95.9% break-even**. The academic tennis literature points the same way: the favourite-longshot bias is larger between lower-ranked players ([Lahvička 2013](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47905/1/MPRA_paper_47905.pdf); [Abinzano, Muga & Santamaría 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v23y2016i8p605-608.html)), but a 40-rule test with data-snooping corrections concludes the tennis market "appears to be much more efficient than suggested by previous studies" ([Lyócsa & Výrost 2018](https://muni.cz/en/research/publications/1397002)), and no study of table-tennis pricing exists at all.

Two features of these leagues make them worse, not better, for a slow taker. In tennis and table tennis the price moves every point and the maker reprices in seconds, so the one-minute stale-limit replay filled only **46% of table-tennis crossings and 60–65% elsewhere**, and the filled subset is the adversely selected one (the ask the poller saw survives only when the favourite has just lost a point); the winner's ask sits at or above 0.95 for a median of **0–1 minutes in Setka Cup** before the tape goes terminal, shorter than the poll interval plus order latency, versus 2–8 minutes in tennis where the late-phase edge at 0.95 is −3.5 ± 2.2. The integrity picture compounds it: **44 of the ITIA's 68 match alerts in 2025 came from ITF M15/M25/W15–W75 events**, none from the ATP Tour ([ITIA Annual Review 2025](https://itia.tennis/media/fohfcuoy/itia_annualreview2025_final.pdf)); New Jersey suspended all Ukrainian table-tennis wagering including Setka Cup after a 2020 integrity alert ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/general/news/new-jersey-suspends-ukrainian-table-tennis-betting-over-match-fixing-concerns)); ESIC's CS2 sanctions in 2025–26 are almost entirely tier 2 and 3, including a lifetime ban tied to a high-value handicap wager ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/counter-strike-2/cs2-pro-banned-for-life-after-match-fixing-scandal-linked-to-4-5-million-bet-3419204/)). Informed flow in these tiers lays the favourite; a bot that buys the favourite 45 seconds after the book moved is its natural counterparty. The tape shows the extreme case: ITF markets left open for hours after the match ("dead book") priced the eventual loser at 80–97¢ a third of the time, losing **−31 to −35 points** at any spread.

Capacity is not the constraint and edge is. $25 orders are invisible against 10^5 resting shares, but at −2 to +1 points the expected value of a $25 order is between −50¢ and +25¢. Taking the two positive cells at face value, the venue produced 5–11 tight in-match crossings per league per week (T20I 5.4, EFL Championship 10.7), of which a one-minute-late limit filled 3.9 and 6.6, so $25 per fill at +2 to +4 points is roughly **$2–11 a week** with a realistic chance the edge is zero. The one lead worth a cheap follow-up is pregame, where the venue's kick-off calibration tilts the other way from the literature: ATP/WTA favourites priced 0.6–0.7 won 59.7% against a 66.7 cost (n = 186, SE 3.6), ITF 0.6–0.7 favourites under-delivered by 13.0 ± 5.5 points (n = 83), and Setka 0.6–0.7 by 9.6 ± 4.5 (n = 122). If that reverse bias holds on the full 160,000-event listing it argues for buying the venue's pregame longshot, which a taker at the ask can do; at current sample sizes it is a hypothesis, not a rule.

## Midterm books are fair and thin ladders leak only single-digit dollars

The non-sport side of the venue is one event wearing many slugs. Of **4,966 open events on 24 October, 1,539 were non-sport**, carrying 10,584 markets of which 81% were midterm contracts: 455 House winner events, 409 House margin-of-victory ladders, Senate and governor winners, turnout thresholds, closest-race contests, seat-count and ballot-measure markets, plus 49 Oscars and 43 Grammys events, BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP ladders, FOMC, CPI, jobs and daily-temperature buckets ([gateway events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?closed=false&limit=500&offset=0)). Liquidity is a cliff. House control quotes **0.905/0.906 with 5.4 million shares bid and 5.3 million offered**; nominee winner markets are 1–2¢ wide with tens of thousands of shares; margin ladders have a 17¢ median spread and are one-sided or unquoted in 78% of markets; Oscars median 19¢; Grammys 97% unquoted ([usho BBO](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/paccc-usho-midterms-2026-11-03-dem/bbo)). Only the first two groups can host a sub-cent strategy; everything else starts a taker 8–20¢ behind.

The strategy a worker would naturally reach for here is buying 95–99¢ favourites, and the best evidence says that trade is fair at the quote. Across 313,972 Kalshi prices, high-price contracts "win more often and yield small positive returns" pre-fee, with a bias slope that is insignificant for Politics (0.022, s.e. 0.021) and Entertainment ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)); across 588 million global Polymarket trades, purchases at or above 90¢ earn **+0.83% per dollar** before fees ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12878)); but a 46,168-market study that priced the trade at the Kalshi quote with the 0.07·P(1−P) fee found **90–95¢ Yes at one day out returned −0.2% (CI −2.8% to +2.1%)** and "no price bucket has a significantly positive return" ([prediction-market-calibration results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration/main/reports/results.md)). On this venue the arithmetic is unforgiving: a House favourite bought at 98¢ nets 1.86¢ if right, about **$2.50 per cycle on the whole account**, locked until accelerated settlement in mid-November or the listed 15 January 2027 expiry, and one loser costs 52 winners; nothing has settled on the venue except primaries, so there is no local data to size the tail ([House product filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/ptc/ptc08062614399.pdf)). Sub-$1 nominee pairs exist (6 of 427 House races, the best 0.97 for a guaranteed $1) but pay under 1% a month and carry legging risk between two IOC legs.

The BTC 15-minute markets look like the cleanest certainty trade and are the clearest failure. Across 260 settled windows the side priced ≥0.99 won **246 of 246 at 60 seconds before close** and 174/174 at 120 seconds, while the 0.95–0.98 side won 66/70 at 300 seconds and 12/13 at 60 seconds ([price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history?symbol=cpc-btc-updown-15m-2026-10-06-1930z&fixedInterval=INTERVAL_ALL&fidelity=60)). But live polling of two windows every 4–10 seconds showed why none of this is harvestable: from 23 seconds to 3 seconds left the winner's book was **bid 0.99 × 63,000 shares with zero offered**, and in the next window the winning No side had 79,000–100,000 shares of demand and not one seller for the entire final three minutes; the last executable ask on the favourite was 0.98 for 13,000–15,000 shares at 30–145 seconds out, where the measured win rate (93–100%, n ≤ 72) straddles the 98.14% break-even and the observed reversal rate from 0.98 at 60 seconds was 1 in 13. The structural reason is that settlement is a 60-observation BRTI average over the final minute ([Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs.md)), so certainty arrives for everyone at once and the only resting liquidity is on the wrong side for a taker. The same dynamic should be expected on election night and at macro prints: by the time a 45-second poll sees 99¢, the offer is gone.

Two genuine but small mechanical leaks remain. The first is stale resting bids inside illiquid threshold ladders. One snapshot of 77 `gte`/`gt` ladders found six monotonicity violations, two of them fee-positive and one surviving an hour: core-CPI ">2.9%" asked at 4¢ while ">3.0%" bid 9¢, 2,700 shares each, about **4.2¢ net on a 95¢ outlay**, in markets with 13 and 61 shares ever traded ([core CPI BBO](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/cpic-uscpicore-october-yoy-2026-11-10-gt3pt0pct/bbo)). This is real, mechanical and fee-positive when the gross gap exceeds about 2.5¢, but it needs two legs (buy the lower threshold's Yes, buy the higher threshold's No), appears a few times a month across the `hrep`, `senate`, `uscpi`, `usunemp`, `usbirth` families, and pays single-digit dollars per hit at $25 clips. The second is the post-release whipsaw in macro ladders: when June 2026 CPI printed 3.5% against a 3.8% consensus ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_07142026.htm)), the ">3.6%" contract went **0.99 → 0.92 → 0.50 → back to 0.98 for three minutes → 0.01**, and ">3.8%" sat near 0.40 for nine minutes before decaying ([CPI price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history?symbol=cpic-uscpi-june-yoy-2026-07-14-gt3pt6pct&fidelity=60&fixedInterval=INTERVAL_ALL)); the FOMC and most jobs legs repriced inside the release minute. A worker could have sold worthless contracts into that print for minutes, but only one that parses the BLS release itself, understands strict-versus-inclusive thresholds, and sells (buys the complementary No), against depth of a few thousand shares and about eight releases a year. Awards markets, turnout and margin ladders and the Chicago-style empty-book 0.99 placeholders (three buckets of one temperature event all printing 0.99 on a book summing to 2.9) are not opportunities; weather favourites are documented as overpriced in the hours before the daily peak on the global venue, with the authors stating their sell rule is not shown executable ([arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13593)), and on this venue 95–98¢ temperature prints 20 hours before the end won only 5 of 9.

## Polymarket US tracks Kalshi within the minute and leads global Polymarket in play

The cross-venue question has a clean answer: Polymarket US is not the slow venue in any contract type the worker could trade. Pregame, across 88 fixtures sampled on 13 October 2026, polymarket.us sat a **median 0.59 points from Pinnacle's de-vigged fair line** with a 0.99% two-way margin, identical to polymarket.com (0.59) and tighter than Kalshi (0.75), against Pinnacle's own 4.02% margin ([OddsPapi developer guide](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/)). The repository's own DraftKings captures agree: over 29 NHL away-side quotes on 23–24 October, |PMUS mid − DK de-vigged fair| had a median of 0.36¢, and only **1 of 29 quotes had the PMUS ask at least 1¢ below DK fair after the fee**. The documented "buy below consensus" edge of 3.5–9.9% was earned against soft bookmakers who then limited the accounts ([Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)); applied to a 1%-margin exchange inside its own tick and fee, nothing is left. A Pinnacle-inclusive feed from The Odds API costs $30–$59 a month at a 60-second pre-match refresh ([the-odds-api.com](https://the-odds-api.com/)), which is useful for measurement and useless as a trading signal against a venue that reprices within the minute.

In play the direction of lag is the opposite of what the worker would need. Over four games on 23 October, **PMUS and Kalshi one-minute changes correlated 0.964–0.994 at lag zero with a maximum gap of 1.5¢**, while global Polymarket's changes correlated 0.946 with PMUS's *previous* minute in Bulls–Bucks, 16–27% of in-play minutes showed |PMUS − global| ≥ 3¢ with extremes of 12–18¢, and on every 2¢-plus global move PMUS had already made half the move a median **4–6 minutes earlier** ([PMUS price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history); [CLOB prices-history](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history); [Kalshi candlesticks](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-market-candlesticks)). The academic lead-lag finding that Kalshi leads sports while global Polymarket leads politics, with leadership following volume and large-trade flow ([Ng, Peng, Tao & Zhou](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)), fits: PMUS and Kalshi are the two US-regulated books where the sports flow is, and both run ahead of the offshore venue. Reading polymarket.com in play would feed the worker a lagging signal. The participants who already exploit in-play latency do so on data that arrives 100–800 ms after the event ([SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/latestnews/2026/05/20/cost-stream-delay-live-betting/)); the one documented large in-play profit on Polymarket, about $208,000 in three months on LoL and Dota 2 from $900, came from ingesting official match data faster than the market with prices that "often lag behind by a matter of seconds," and its author expected it to decay ([esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/polymarket-bot-makes-over-200k-in-3-months-with-lol-dota-2-esports/)). A 45-second poller is one to two orders of magnitude behind that.

Politics and macro show the same picture at a slower clock. Over 14 days and 20,160 minutes per pair, the October FOMC "no change" contract on PMUS stayed within a **median 0.5¢ of both global Polymarket and Kalshi**, with gaps of 3¢ or more in 1.7% of minutes against global and 0.8% against Kalshi, almost all on 14 October when the contract swung roughly 30 points; PMUS reached half of each 2¢-plus Kalshi move at a median of 0 minutes and of each global move at a median of 1–7 minutes, sometimes before global ([PMUS Fed price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history?symbol=rdc-usfed-fomc-2026-10-28-nochng&fidelity=1)). Where PMUS does differ persistently it is not a lag: Senate control sat **2.3¢ below global for the whole 14 days** and House control 0.9¢ below, a clientele or definition gap that never converges and that a US person cannot hedge because no account can hold both books ([ThinBlimp](https://thinblimp.com/guides/polymarket-us-vs-polymarket)). BTC "above X" ladders repriced in the same minute as Coinbase spot (change correlation 0.49–0.73 at lag zero, a residual 0.2–0.5 at one to two minutes) and settle on BRTI rather than any exchange print ([Coinbase candles](https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/BTC-USD/candles?granularity=60); [Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs.md)).

Adding Kalshi to the worker is technically easy and economically empty. Kalshi's public REST market data needs no key, its 1-minute candles carry bid/ask OHLC, and its Basic tier allows about 20 reads and 10 writes per second, though keyless polling drew 429s after a handful of requests ([Kalshi rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)); its taker fee is 0.07 × P × (1 − P), marginally above PMUS. A two-legged PMUS/Kalshi trade on a 1¢-wide contract at 0.92 needs a gap above roughly 3.3¢ (two spreads plus two fees of about 0.63¢) before it earns anything, which occurred in under 1% of minutes and closed within minutes; a worker reacting on its next poll might capture 1–3¢ on a $25 order, **$0.25–$0.90 per hit, a few hits a month per contract**. Nothing found prohibits a US person from holding accounts on both DCMs or from reading polymarket.com, whose own banner offers US visitors "Continue in view only mode" ([polymarket.com/tos](https://polymarket.com/tos)).

## Market making and Dutch books pay nothing; the finish line pays one to three cents

Market making is the extension most often proposed for a bot that loses as a taker, and on this venue it has no income source for a small account. Tier 1 makers (below $10M of prior-month maker volume) pay no fee and receive no rebate ([Fee Schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)); the Market Maker Program requires a signed agreement, approval "in its sole discretion," and **at least 5% of a Series' total maker notional in the month**, with its share tiers redacted ([CFTC filing 09302630733](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09302630733.pdf)). The only income is therefore the spread, which pregame on the big US leagues is one tick in **57% (CBB) to 86% (CFB) of minutes**, and the best published evidence on prediction-market makers is that over 300,000 Kalshi contracts **"makers have an average loss of about 10%"** against 32% for takers: making beats taking without being positive ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). The 45-second loop is adequate for pregame quote management, where the long ask moves 2¢ or more in at most 0.3% of minutes, and hopeless in play, where it moves 2¢ or more in 16–30% of minutes and 5¢ or more in 12–23% of final-15-minute minutes ([gateway price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history)), which is the same pattern as the user's standing-order backtest. A pregame-only, post-only quoter with inventory caps is a −$2 to +$1 a day activity whose variance is set by line-aware bettors picking off a one-tick quote; the practitioner who kept a maker bot alive on the global venue after February 2026 reports **EV of about $0.03 per trade at 78 trades a day** ([dev.to](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)).

Dutch books and consistency trades do not exist at 45-second resolution. In the live snapshot, **none of 13,909 two-sided markets had long ask plus short ask below 1.00**; of 468 three-way soccer sets, 11 showed an after-fee sum-to-one profit on the listing's quotes and every one vanished on an immediate per-slug re-read, the listing's `updatedAt` being up to an hour stale; across six game ladders with 4,740 ordered line pairs there was one dominance violation (Spurs +1.5 bid 0.88 above Spurs +2.5 ask 0.87), worth −0.5¢ after two taker fees ([gateway events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?closed=false&limit=500&offset=0)). The global literature explains the residue: single-market YES/NO anomalies in live NBA markets had a **median life of 3.6 seconds**, moneyline-versus-spread episodes 16 seconds with an average executable size of 14.8 shares and 76.9% unable to absorb $100 ([Cheng, Yang & Zou](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864)); NegRisk conversion profit per trade fell from about $1 in 2024 to about $0.08 in early 2026 ([Gebele, Mutzel & Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666v1)); cross-venue Kalshi–Polymarket gaps had a median life of 9 seconds with 96% closed inside 30 ([dev.to log](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf)). A 45-second loop sees roughly one in a few hundred of these windows and none of their depth. Other folk patterns (post-listing mispricing, overnight thinness, round numbers, "resolution dump") either cannot be measured from the tape or reduce to strategies already tested: pregame wide-or-empty books are 2–5% of minutes through the US day, and the "resolution dump" is simply the 0.99 ask discussed next.

That 0.99 ask is the one structural trade with a measured positive expectation. On **8,016 settled major-league games** with a recorded finish and a tape, the winner's ask was still at or below 0.99 after the recorded finish in **43% of games (3,426), at or below 0.98 in 18% (1,429) and at or below 0.97 in 11.5% (922)**, covering 26,943 post-finish game-minutes; the first sub-1.00 minute arrives a median 0.25 minutes after the finish, the ask typically reaches 1.00 within about a minute (median 0.77 minutes, 90th percentile 1.38), but in 22% of NBA, 34% of NHL, 31% of NFL and 55% of CFB games it had still not reached 1.00 when the 15-minute tape ended, and tennis never settled inside the tape ([gateway price history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history); cache at `data/raw/polymarket_us/history/`). Net of fee the trade pays **0.94% at 0.99, 1.90% at 0.98 and 2.88% at 0.97** per cycle, so break-even requires the "decided" side to lose less than 0.93%, 1.86% or 2.80% of the time. The measured rate at which the market priced the loser at 90¢ or better for two minutes after the finish was **4 in 9,273 games (0.04%)**, and of those four, one is a crossed non-tradeable snapshot, one is a frozen dead tape, one is incoherent, and only the 2026-04-04 Canadiens–Devils flip could be a genuine reversal or a side-mapping error. The loser was separately offered at 0.90–0.995 for three minutes or more after the finish in 13 games (0.14%), ten of them ITF tennis, which is why the rule must require both the winner's ask ≤ 0.99 and the loser's ask ≤ 0.02 rather than reading one side. A larger window precedes the finish: in 4,441 of 9,273 games the loser's ask was already ≤ 0.01 for a **median 6 minutes (90th percentile 187) before the recorded finish**, with the winner at 0.99 in 83% of those minutes; that tail carries the residual game risk the favorites backtest already priced at roughly break-even, so the clean version of the trade waits for an independent final signal.

What the worker needs is modest and specific. The existing IOC-at-the-ask path is the right execution. It needs a "final" signal that is not the venue's `finishedTimestamp` (median 2.7 minutes late), so either a score feed or the event's `live`/`period` fields read every poll ([Events & Markets](https://docs.polymarket.us/concepts/events-and-markets.md)); the two-sided ask rule above; a `/book` or WebSocket depth read to size the clip, because the tape is top-of-book and two-decimal, so a "0.99 ask" may be a single small order or a 0.9925 rounded down; and per-league tracking of settlement latency, since capital sits locked from fill until Polymarket Clearing posts the result, which the docs leave unquantified ([Market Settlement](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/markets/contract-settlement.md)) and which caps a $135 account at about five concurrent $25 clips. The risks are a $0.50 tie settlement (0.21% of settlements, avoidable by the loser-ask rule, since a tied game is not priced at 0.99 at the buzzer), last-fair-market-price voids on no-contests, "result under review" delays, a premature final signal of the worker's own making, partial fills, and competition for the 0.98s. Capacity, not edge, bounds the dollars: CBB in season settles a median 32.5 games a day (83 at the 90th percentile), NBA 6, NHL 5, MLB 12, CFB 3 (65 on Saturdays), NFL 1 (10 on Sundays); at 43% with a sub-1.00 ask and $25 a clip, CBB alone offers about 13 opportunities and up to $325 deployed a day for **at most $3 a day gross, with a strong multi-sport day near $5–7 and a typical day $1–3**, near zero in summer. The 0.98s (18% of games, 1.9%) are where the money is and where competition should be assumed.

The ordering rests on one principle the data make unavoidable: with a fee of 0.0695·p(1−p), any pattern worth 1.7¢ or less per contract at mid prices is unprofitable for a taker, and only the far tail above 97¢, where the fee falls to 0.07–0.20¢, is cheap enough to trade on crumbs. That is why the single surviving strategy lives at the finish line, and why it should be built as the venue's own tape describes it rather than as an annualised yield: a cycle at 0.94% would annualise to thousands of percent if capital could be redeployed continuously, and it cannot, because each fill sits locked for an unmeasured interval and the opportunity count is set by the sports calendar. The first instrumented weeks should measure three things the notes could not: depth behind the 0.98–0.99 asks via `/book`, minutes from fill to credited settlement per league via the private WebSocket's POSITION and ACCOUNT_BALANCE streams ([WebSocket Overview](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/websocket/overview.md)), and the share of fills that were partial. If the 0.98s turn out to be a few shares deep or already gone, the honest expectation collapses toward the 0.99s at about 0.9%, and the strategy becomes a $1-a-day curiosity that mostly teaches the worker's plumbing.

Two infrastructure changes are worth making regardless of strategy, because they correct errors in the current loop rather than add edge. Replace the cached gateway poll with the authenticated market-data WebSocket, which removes the 30-second cache and the up-to-75-second staleness from every decision the worker makes; and read `feeCoefficient` per market, log every order, cancel and fill with timestamps, and wire a kill switch and price collar, which the Rulebook requires of automated systems and which costs an afternoon ([Rulebook](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules03202641475.pdf)). Do not run a second account or a friend's to provide liquidity to the first; self-matching across commonly controlled accounts is prohibited however the orders are entered, and the market-maker programme's surveillance explicitly flags related-account trading.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the question from "which neglected corner of the venue is slow" to "where is the fee small enough that fair pricing still leaves a crumb." Every corner the user named turned out to be fast: the smallest ITF and Setka Cup books are quoted by the same class of feed-driven professional that quotes the NFL, Polymarket US is itself the price leader against the offshore venue in play, and the thin non-sport ladders are thin in spread rather than stale in level. The venue's microstructure is designed for this outcome, since its liquidity programmes pay professionals per second to sit near the touch, its taker fee funds their rebates, and its 30-second edge cache ensures that anyone reading the free gateway is behind them by construction. What survives is not an informational edge but a time-value one: holders who want their capital back before clearing sell the decided side for a cent or two, and a patient, correctly gated buyer can collect it at a measured surprise rate far below break-even.

The implication for the worker's design is that speed upgrades buy correctness, not opportunity. Moving to the WebSocket, reading depth and fees per market, and gating on an independent final signal are what make the one real trade executable and keep the rest from losing money by accident; none of them turns the worker into a competitor with the sub-second participants who own the Dutch books, the 15-minute BTC windows and the first seconds after a goal. The account's realistic ceiling is a few dollars a day in the college-basketball season, which is a fine payoff for a system that is otherwise learning how the venue clears and settles, and a poor one for any plan that expects the $135 to compound. The leads worth a cheap second look are the full-listing test of the pregame reverse favourite-longshot tilt in tennis and table tennis, and the first election night on this venue, whose post-call liquidity has never been observed and is the one place a model-free yield on non-sport contracts could still appear.
