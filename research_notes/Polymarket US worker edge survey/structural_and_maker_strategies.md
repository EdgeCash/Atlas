Headline (one paragraph for the writer). On this venue a $135 account with a 45-second top-of-book poller has exactly one structural trade with a measured positive expectation: buying the already-decided side at 0.98–0.99 in the minutes around the recorded finish ("settlement yield"), worth about 0.9–1.9% per cycle after fee with a measured surprise rate at least an order of magnitude below break-even, but capacity-bound to a few dollars a day and dependent on depth the tape cannot see. Market making earns no rebate below $10M/month of maker volume, the venue's maker programme requires a signed agreement and ≥5% of a Series' maker volume, and the published evidence (Kalshi, >300k contracts) is that makers lose on average, less than takers. Dutch books do not exist at 45-second resolution: zero crossed two-sided books in 13,909 quoted markets, zero after-fee sum-to-one violations that survived a re-read, one spread-ladder dominance violation worth 0.2¢ in 4,740 pairs, and the global literature puts episode half-lives at 3.6–16 seconds.

## Key question 1: Market making on Polymarket US — maker incentives, comparison with global Polymarket and Kalshi, practitioner evidence, viability of pregame-only quoting on a 45-second loop

### Takeaway
There is no maker rebate for a small account: the live fee schedule pays rebates only from $10M of prior-month maker volume (Tier 2, −0.0035) upward, and the venue's Series-Based Market Maker Program (effective 2026-10-15) is an approved-participant programme that requires ≥5% of a Series' total maker notional per month. A Tier 1 maker pays no fee and earns no rebate, so the only income is the spread, which pregame is ≤1¢ in 57–86% of minutes on the big US leagues; the best published evidence on prediction-market makers (Kalshi, >300k contracts) is an average maker loss of about 10% versus about 32% for takers, i.e. making beats taking but is not positive. Pregame quotes on this venue are almost static (≥2¢ minute-to-minute moves in ≤0.3% of pregame minutes), so a 45-second loop is not the problem; adverse selection and the absence of spread are.

### Inferences
- At Tier 1 the maker's whole income is the spread, which pregame on the big leagues is one tick (1¢; 0.25¢ ticks on NFL, 0.5¢ on CFB) more than half the time. A two-sided quote that fills on both sides nets ≤1¢ per contract; a one-sided fill against a sharper pregame bettor who has a sportsbook line is a loss of several cents. The Kalshi −10% average maker return (global/Kalshi evidence, dominated by retail makers and longshot contracts) is the best available prior; nothing here suggests the US venue's pregame flow is less informed.
- A 45-second poll is adequate for pregame quote management (quotes move ≥2¢ in ≤0.3% of minutes) and inadequate in play (≥2¢ in 16–30% of minutes, ≥5¢ in 12–23% of final-15-minute minutes), which is the same pattern as the user's own standing-order backtest (−4 to −10 points late in games).
- Thin politics/culture markets (median widths 13–50¢) do offer spread to capture, but the flow that crosses a 17¢-wide House-race book is either news-driven or tiny; with no rebate and no depth read the worker would be the stalest quote in the book. Pregame sports quoting at one tick is a near-zero-expectation activity whose variance is set by adverse selection; the realistic range for a $135 account is −$1 to +$1 a day.
- Requirements if attempted anyway: GTC + post-only orders, the `/book` depth endpoint or WebSocket (the listing's `bestBidQuote/bestAskQuote` were stale by up to an hour in the three-way soccer check below), cancel-on-move logic, inventory caps far below $135, and a pregame-only switch keyed to `startTime`.

### Gaps
- The filing that replaced the flat −0.0125 maker rebate with the $10M+ tiers was not found; the docs page is the authority as of 2026-10-24.
- Section 6 (market-share tiers, multipliers, cap) of the Market Maker Program is redacted; no public figure for what a designated maker earns.
- No audited P&L of any quoting bot on any prediction market was found; practitioner claims are anecdotal.
- Kalshi's standard taker coefficient was not re-verified this session (the search confirmed only the 0.0175 maker formula and that most markets carry no maker fee).

## Key question 2: Settlement / resolution yield — how long contracts sit at 97–99¢ after the result, whether an ask below 1.00 persists, implied return, and surprise frequency (measured)

### Takeaway
Measured on 8,016 settled games with a recorded finish and a tape: the winner's ask was still ≤0.99 after the recorded finish in 43% of games (3,426; 26,943 game-minutes), ≤0.98 in 18%, ≤0.97 in 11.5%; the ask typically reaches 1.00 within about a minute of the recorded finish (median 0.7–1.1 min NBA/NHL/CBB/NFL, 3 min CFB/MLB) but in 22–55% of major-league games it had still not reached 1.00 when the 15-minute tape ended. Net of fee the trade pays 0.94% at 0.99, 1.90% at 0.98 and 2.88% at 0.97 per cycle; break-even loss rates are 0.93% / 1.86% / 2.80%, against a measured post-finish "market priced the loser" rate of 4 in 9,273 (0.04%), at least two of which are tape artefacts. The venue's `finishedTimestamp` lags the end of two-sided trading by a median 2.7 minutes (p90 59 min), so "result known" must come from elsewhere.

### Inferences
- Arithmetic per contract: at 0.99 the fee is 0.000688, net gain 0.009312 on 0.990688 at risk = 0.94%; at 0.98: 0.001362 fee, 1.90%; at 0.97: 0.002022 fee, 2.88%. Break-even probability of a zero settlement: 0.93% / 1.86% / 2.80%. A 0.50 tie settlement costs 0.49, i.e. ~53 winning 0.99 trades; ties were 0.21% of settlements but a tied game is not priced at 0.99 at the buzzer, so this risk is avoidable by requiring the loser's ask ≤0.02 as well.
- Annualised return is not the binding number. A cycle (buy in the decided tail or at the finish, settle within 1–15+ minutes) at 0.94% would annualise to thousands of percent if capital could be redeployed continuously; it cannot. Capacity = (games with a sub-1.00 ask) × (depth at that ask, unknown) × ($25 cap). Using the measured frequencies and the user's cap: CBB season ~30 games/day × 43% with a ≤0.99 ask ≈ 13 opportunities/day → ≤$325 deployed → ≤$3/day gross; add NBA/NHL/CFB and a strong day is ~$5–7; a typical day $1–3. The 0.98s (18% of games, 1.9%) are where the money is and where competition should be assumed.
- The decided-but-not-finished tail (median 6 minutes, winner at 0.99 in 83% of it) is a larger window than the post-finish period but carries the residual game risk the user's favorites backtest already priced (about break-even late on a tight book); the clean version of the trade waits for an independent "final" signal. The venue's own `finishedTimestamp` is not usable in real time (median 2.7 min, p90 59 min late versus the tape), so the worker needs a score feed or the event's `live`/`period` fields read every poll, and a rule that both the winner's ask ≤0.99 and the loser's ask ≤0.02 hold.
- Settlement latency is heterogeneous: NBA/CBB usually settle inside two minutes; CFB, NFL, NHL and tennis frequently take longer than the 15-minute tape, and tennis never settled inside it. For a $135 account that means capital locked for an unknown span after each fill, which caps concurrent positions at about five $25 clips.
- Because the tape is top-of-book only and 2-decimal, a "0.99 ask" may be a single small order or a 0.9925 rounded down; expect partial fills at $25.

### Gaps
- Depth at the 0.98–0.99 asks is unobservable in the cache; the `/v1/markets/{slug}/book` endpoint exists but was not sampled post-finish in this session.
- Time from exchange settlement to cash availability was not measured (tapes end 15 min after the finish; the docs say balances update automatically but give no latency).
- The 2026-04-04 Canadiens–Devils flip could not be adjudicated as a real reversal versus a tape error; it is the only candidate genuine post-finish surprise in 9,273 games.
- Correction/void frequency on non-sports contracts (politics, awards) was not measured; the cache is sports moneylines only.

## Key question 3: Dutch books and consistency on the venue right now — two-sided asks summing below 1.00 plus fees, mutually exclusive outcome sets summing below one, moneyline versus spread ladder (measured)

### Takeaway
In the live snapshot none of 13,909 two-sided markets had long ask + short ask below 1.00 (zero crossed books; 57.8% were exactly one tick wide); among 468 three-way soccer sets only 11 showed an after-fee sum-to-one profit on the listing's quotes and all were stale, vanishing on an immediate re-read by slug; exhaustive futures sets carry overrounds of 5–49% on the ask side; across six game ladders (36–42 lines each, 4,740 ordered line pairs) there was one dominance violation (Spurs +1.5 bid 0.88 above Spurs +2.5 ask 0.87), worth −0.5¢ after two taker fees and +0.2¢ if one leg rests. A taker on this venue needs a combined gap of ≥3.5¢ at mid prices to pay two fees; nothing close was observed.

### Inferences
- On the US venue the structural minimum cost of a two-leg taker Dutch book is two fees: 3.5¢ combined at p≈0.5, 1.4¢ at p≈0.9/0.1. The widest observed inconsistency in 4,740 ladder pairs was 1¢ gross; the snapshot has no two-sided market inside 1¢ of crossing. A 45-second poller sees only the residue after sub-second participants, and the residue measured here is zero.
- Buy-all-NO across exhaustive futures (division winners, governor races) is the only consistency trade with multi-cent gross gaps on the listing (e.g. NBA division bids summing to 0.64–0.95 imply NO baskets priced well over fair), and the sign is the wrong way: the books are overround on both sides, as a market with wide one-sided quotes should be.
- The stale-listing finding matters for the worker's own design: any Dutch-book or stale-quote signal must be confirmed from `/v1/markets/{slug}/book` (or the WebSocket) before an IOC is sent; the listing's quote age (`updatedAt`) can exceed an hour.
- "House control vs district races" type consistency (politics) was not computable without a model of which districts decide control; the venue lists 455 `ushr` events, but the listing's House-race books are 17¢ wide at the median, so any inconsistency would be swallowed by the spread.

### Gaps
- Single snapshot; no time series of listing quotes to estimate how often brief crossings occur between polls on this venue.
- Totals-versus-moneyline (team win vs match total) consistency needs a model of score distributions and was not attempted; Cheng et al. also did not analyse it.
- Politics cross-market consistency (control of the House vs district ladders) not measured.

## Key question 4: Related-market consistency over time — do bots on global Polymarket/Kalshi keep gaps closed within seconds, leaving a 45-second poller crumbs?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the evidence is quantitative: median violation lifetimes of 3.6 s (single-market, NBA in play), 16 s (moneyline-vs-spread and NegRisk YES-side), 8 s (NegRisk NO-side), executable sizes around 15 shares, and per-trade arbitrage profit on global Polymarket falling from ≈$1 to ≈$0.08 between 2024 and early 2026; cross-venue gaps median 9 s. A 45-second loop sees roughly one in a few hundred of these windows and none of their depth.

### Cited Findings
- Global Polymarket NBA, Feb–Mar 2026: in-game combinatorial episodes median 16.0 s, 17.2% lasted ≤4.0 s; single-market episodes median 3.614 s; pregame episodes 11 in 173 games (0.0034% of time) — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864)
- Global Polymarket NegRisk: YES-side median 16.15 s, NO-side 7.99 s; conversion profit per trade ≈$0.08 in early 2026; "all-taker conversions are less frequent but larger on average, and … maker-assisted share is rising" — [arXiv 2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666v1)
- Global Polymarket 2024–25: profits concentrated in bot-like accounts (top 10 ≈21%); "about 1% of the U.S. election opportunities were exploited" — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1)
- Global Polymarket removed a built-in 500 ms taker delay in February 2026, which a bot author says closed the entry window latency bots had used (anecdotal) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5); Polymarket moved to CTF Exchange V2 / pUSD on April 28, 2026 — [Dwellir](https://www.dwellir.com/blog/best-polygon-rpc-for-polymarket)
- Cross-venue Kalshi–Polymarket gaps: 870 per 24 h, median 9 s, 96% closed inside 30 s (single unaudited log) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf); practitioner essay on execution speed gives no data — [casatrick substack](https://casatrick.substack.com/p/polymarket-arbitrage-bot-execution-speed)
- Measured on the US venue: the one ladder violation found (1¢ gross) and the 11 stale three-way sets that disappeared on re-read are consistent with the venue having the same fast participants; the US fee schedule's 25 req/s REST limit and WebSocket market data are available to anyone — [Rate Limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rate-limits.md)

### Inferences
- Even with a WebSocket and sub-second orders, the measured executable size (≈15 shares, 77% of episodes unable to absorb $100) and the US venue's two-sided fee make this a sub-$1-per-episode trade; at $1–25 order sizes the worker could not scale it even if it were fast.
- The user's "one in five crossings on a book wider than 4¢" is the same phenomenon seen from the other side: at 45-second resolution the worker mostly sees books after the fast money has left or before it has arrived.

### Gaps
- No study of the US venue's order flow latency or bot population exists; the inference that the same participants operate there rests on the identical API shape and the measured absence of gaps.

## Key question 5: Other documented mechanical patterns — post-listing mispricing, overnight/low-liquidity hours, round numbers, "resolution dump", and whether any survive the 0.0695 × p × (1 − p) fee

### Takeaway
None of these has venue-specific evidence that survives the fee. Post-listing mispricing cannot be measured from the cache (tapes start 2 h before the game) and the literature only says "thin, young markets" are shallow; time-of-day effects pregame on this venue are small (wide or empty books 2–5% of minutes through the US day, 12–23% before 10:00 ET on tiny samples); round-number clustering is unobservable at 2-decimal top-of-book; the "resolution dump" is an anecdote globally and is simply the 0.99 ask measured in Key question 2; the favourite-longshot bias is well documented (Kalshi <10¢ contracts lose heavily) but its tradeable inverse is the favorites rule the user already backtested to a loss mid-game.

### Cited Findings
- Favourite-longshot: Kalshi contracts priced below 10¢ "generate very large average losses"; the bias is stronger for takers — [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market); football betting 12,084 matches (2017): favourites −3.64%, outsiders −26.08%; PredictIt/IEM 2016 sum-to-one deviations up to 55¢ driven by fees and limits — [Quantpedia survey](https://quantpedia.com/systematic-edges-in-prediction-markets/); fees on winnings themselves generate a favourite-longshot pattern — [UCD working paper (via search)](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/items/1f52bf3c-4003-46b2-836b-e623f5aeaff9); the bias's level varies over a contract's life and is consistent with herding in the last trading days — [Restocchi, McGroarty, Gerding 2018](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/423232/2/1_s2.0_S1544612318303349_main.pdf)
- Young markets: on-chain analysis of 249 Polymarket 2024 election markets finds naive volume overstates turnover most "in thin, young markets" (global) — [arXiv 2603.03136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136); "market makers, bots, and traders are still adjusting strategies and deploying liquidity to these thinner books" for newly listed contracts (global, Dune) — [Dune blog](https://dune.com/blog/polymarket-fast-markets)
- Measured, pregame time of day (ET ≈ UTC−4; share of pregame minutes with no quote or width >4¢): 10:00 5.4% (n=10,130), 11:00 3.2%, 12:00 4.2%, 13:00 3.6%, 14:00 2.6%, 15:00 2.9%, 16:00 3.1%, 17:00 3.6%, 18:00 3.4% (170,774), 19:00 3.1% (176,916), 20:00 3.4%, 21:00 2.3%, 22:00 1.8%, 23:00 2.0%; 08:00 12.5% (n=240), 09:00 22.7% (n=630). By half-hours before the start the share is flat at 3.1–3.4% — measured from `history/*.json`
- Measured, price granularity: of the long-ask values on the first 500 tapes, 98,962 had a third decimal of 0 and 46,277 of 9 (0.999/0.989 forms), the rest spread thinly; the tape is effectively 2-decimal, so round-number clustering of resting orders cannot be read from it — same source
- "Resolution dump": a quoted trader says retail "rush to cash out near $0.997" (global, anecdotal); a claimed manipulation playbook pushes 0.99 to 0.90 then buys back (unverified) — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/polymarket-arbitrage-risk-free-profit/); [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/28555). A bot that always buys NO on global Polymarket is reported profitable because "nothing ever happens" (anecdotal) — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364381/polymarket-bot-bets-no-has-a-point)
- Measured, what the venue's post-result tape shows: the 0.99/0.01 state (someone offering the winner at 0.99 while nobody bids above 0.99) is the book in 17% of games 14 minutes after the recorded finish and in 83% of decided-but-not-finished minutes (Key question 2)

### Inferences
- With the fee shape 0.0695·p(1−p), any pattern worth ≤1.7¢ per contract at mid prices is unprofitable for a taker and any pattern worth ≤0.14¢ at 98¢ is unprofitable; only the far tails (≥97¢) are cheap enough to trade on crumbs, which is why the only surviving strategy in this note lives there.
- Pregame overnight thinness exists (the 08:00–09:00 ET rows) but with 240–630 minutes of sample it is a curiosity, and a maker exploiting it would be quoting into the hours when sportsbook line moves arrive.
- A "buy the 0.99 when holders dump for liquidity" rule is the settlement-yield trade; a "sell the longshot" rule is the favorites rule; neither is a separate edge.

### Gaps
- No first-minutes-after-listing data on this venue (the tapes begin 2 h pre-game; futures/politics listings were not tracked).
- No depth or resting-order data, so round-number clustering and "resolution dump" size cannot be measured here.

## Key question 6: Realistic capacity — dollars a day a $135 account could deploy in each strategy and at what expected return (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Settlement yield: $100–750 of $25 clips a day in season, +0.9–1.9% per clip, $1–7 a day gross, low variance, capacity set by unseen depth and by competition for the 0.98s; everything else is zero or negative at this order size and polling speed.

### Cited Findings
- Per-strategy figures above; fee arithmetic: fee 0.000688 at 0.99, 0.001362 at 0.98, 0.002022 at 0.97, 0.0174 at 0.50 — [Polymarket US Fee Schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)
- Measured opportunity counts (Key question 2): ≤0.99 post-finish ask in 43% of games, ≤0.98 in 18%, ≤0.97 in 11.5%; CBB median 32.5 settled games/day in season, NBA 6, NHL 5, MLB 12, CFB 3 (65 on Saturdays), NFL 1 (10 on Sundays)
- Global executable size for Dutch books ≈14.8 shares and 77% of episodes under $100 — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864)
- Kalshi makers −10% average return — [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market)

### Inferences (per strategy: mechanism, evidence, risks, worker requirements, estimate)
1. Market making (two-sided GTC quotes). Mechanism: earn the spread between bid and ask; no rebate at Tier 1, no maker fee either. Evidence: pregame spreads ≤1¢ 57–86% of minutes; Kalshi makers −10% average; no positive bot P&L published. Risks: adverse selection by line-aware pregame bettors; in play, 16–30% of minutes move ≥2¢ (pick-offs on a 45-s loop guaranteed); inventory accumulates on the wrong side (the user's standing-order backtest −4 to −10 points). Requires: GTC + post-only, `/book` depth or WebSocket, cancel/replace within seconds, pregame-only gating, inventory caps. Estimate: turnover $50–200/day at best, expectation −1% to +0.5% of turnover → about −$2 to +$1/day; not recommended without a rebate tier that is 5 orders of magnitude away.
2. Settlement yield (buy the decided side at 0.97–0.99). Mechanism: collect the last 1–3¢ plus the time value others pay to exit before clearing. Evidence: measured 0.94/1.90/2.88% per cycle; surprise rate ≤0.04% (likely lower; artefacts) vs 0.93% break-even; opportunities in 43%/18%/11.5% of games. Risks: 0.50 tie settlements (0.21% of games, avoidable by requiring loser ask ≤0.02), LFMP voids, "result under review" delays locking capital, a premature "final" signal (the venue's timestamp is late, not early, but the worker's own signal might be early), partial fills, competition taking the 0.98s. Requires: the existing IOC-at-ask path, an independent final-score signal (or the event's `live`/`period` fields each poll), a rule on both sides' asks, a `/book` read to size the clip, tracking of settlement latency per league. Estimate: $100–750/day deployable in CBB/NBA/NHL season at $25 clips, +$1–7/day gross, roughly +1–4% of the account per day on busy days, near zero in summer; the single credible structural trade here.
3. Dutch books (two sides; exhaustive sets; ladders). Mechanism: buy all outcomes below $1 or sell above. Evidence: zero in 13,909 markets; 0 of 468 three-way sets on re-read; 1 of 4,740 ladder pairs at 1¢ gross; global lifetimes 3.6–16 s and ≈15-share depth. Risks: legging (non-atomic), fee on both legs (≥3.5¢ at mid), stale listing quotes. Requires: WebSocket, sub-second two-leg execution, per-slug ladder reads (one request per game). Estimate: $0/day.
4. Post-result stale quotes (asks below 1.00 after the result). Mechanism: identical to 2 but targeting the laggards; the measured population is 0.99 in 17% of games 14 minutes after the finish and 0.98 in ~2%. Risks and requirements as in 2 plus the lesson that the loser's side also shows stale 0.90–0.995 asks in 0.14% of games (13 games), which a result-confused bot would buy. Estimate: subsumed in 2.
5. Other patterns (listing, time of day, round numbers, resolution dump, longshot bias). Evidence: none venue-specific that clears the fee; time-of-day wide-book share 2–5%. Estimate: $0/day; the longshot inverse is the favorites rule already tested.

### Gaps
- All capacity figures assume the visible top-of-book ask has ≥25 contracts behind it; this is unmeasured and probably often false at 0.98.
- Whether makers pay nothing at Tier 1 is read off the schedule's absence of a maker-fee line, not a sentence saying so; the other researcher's check should confirm it.
- No measurement of how quickly settled cash becomes buying power, which decides how many $25 clips can run concurrently.
