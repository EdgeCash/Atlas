# Non-sport event contracts on Polymarket US: what is listed, how efficiently it is priced, and whether a 45-second $1–$25 taker bot has a mechanical edge

Scope: Polymarket US (QCX LLC d/b/a Polymarket US, the CFTC-designated contract market; gateway `gateway.polymarket.us`), read on 24–25 October 2026 (listing pulled 23:3x UTC on 24 Oct). "Measured" means computed from the gateway or the repository's cached closed-event listing; "literature" means published studies of other venues (Kalshi, global Polymarket, PredictIt, Intrade, IEM), labelled as such. Scripts and raw pulls are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Atlas/8739734e-beef-5b53-b4a5-62b091fed4fa/scratchpad/` (files `pm/open_nonsport.json`, `pm/btc15_big.json`, `pm/temp_sample.json`, `pm/pol_settled.json`, `pm/live15_log.jsonl`); nothing in the repository was modified.

Fee convention used throughout (confirmed from the venue's CFTC fee filings, see Q9): taker fee = 0.0695 × p × (1 − p) per contract, i.e. 0.069¢ at 99¢, 0.136¢ at 98¢, 0.202¢ at 97¢, 0.33¢ at 95¢, 0.625¢ at 90¢, 1.74¢ at 50¢. Break-even win rates for a buy-and-hold of a favourite at the ask: 99¢ → 99.07%; 98¢ → 98.14%; 97¢ → 97.20%; 95¢ → 95.33%; 90¢ → 90.63%.

## Q2. Favourite–longshot bias at 90–99¢: literature, and what this venue's own settled data show

### Takeaway
The best recent evidence (Kalshi 2021–2026, global Polymarket 2022–2026) says contracts priced 95–99¢ do resolve Yes slightly more often than the price implies (+0.3 to +2.6 percentage points depending on venue and horizon), and the excess concentrates in the last day and hour; but at the quote and after taker fees the return to buying favourites is statistically zero (Kalshi 90–95¢ at 1 day: −0.2%, CI −2.8% to +2.1%). On this venue the only settled high-price contracts with usable histories are BTC 15-minute and daily-temperature buckets; in those the 99¢ side won 246/246 (BTC, 60 s before close) and 50/50 (temperature, 12 h before close), which is consistent with the literature but cannot statistically clear the 99.07% break-even at 99¢.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi, 313,972 Yes/No prices on 46,282 contracts, 2021–April 2025: "low-price contracts win far less often than required to break even after fees, while high-price contracts win more often and yield small positive returns"; 33.8% of all observations are in the 90–99¢ band; "for contracts above 70c, there is evidence of statistically significant, though small, positive post-fee returns"; makers buying ≥50¢ earn 2.6%; fee modelled as $0.07·P(1−P); the pre-fee bias slope is small and insignificant for Politics (0.022, s.e. 0.021) and Entertainment (0.020, s.e. 0.012) versus 0.058 for Crypto and 0.034 for Economics — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers", Jan 2026](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf) (literature).
- Global Polymarket, 588 million trades by 2.48 million wallets, markets resolved by 29 Mar 2026: purchases at or above 90¢ earn +0.83% per dollar pooled (CI 0.60–1.07%) and +0.277% equal-weighted per child market; probability error +0.82 pp pooled; by category, favourites earn +1.04% (Politics, equal-weight) / +1.42% (pooled) and +0.64% / +0.54% (Crypto); returns exclude fees and are not annualised — [Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe, arXiv 2609.12878, Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12878) (literature).
- 46,168 resolved Kalshi + Polymarket markets priced at fixed horizons (Kalshi bid/ask mids, Polymarket hourly prices): Kalshi 1 h before close, 98–100¢ bin n=3,111 mean price 99.4% realised 99.8% (+0.5 pp); 95–98¢ n=433 realised 99.1% vs 96.5% (+2.6 pp); 90–95¢ n=237 94.9% vs 92.7%; at 1 day the 98–100¢ bin (n=1,474) realised 99.8% vs 99.2%; Polymarket 1 h: 98–100¢ n=2,883 realised 99.8% vs 99.7% (+0.1 pp), 95–98¢ n=368 97.0% vs 96.7%; calibration slope rises toward close on Kalshi (1.02 at 30 d, 1.11 at 1 d, 1.22 at 1 h) but Polymarket 2026 is slightly overconfident (0.92 at 1 d) — [hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration results.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration/main/reports/results.md) and [README](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration) (literature, unrefereed).
- Same study, returns from trading at the quote on Kalshi 1 day before close with the 0.07·P(1−P) taker fee: 80–90¢ Yes +1.7% [−1.5%, +4.6%]; 90–95¢ Yes −0.2% [−2.8%, +2.1%]; "favourites or fading longshots lose only 0–2% after the spread and taker fee"; "no price bucket has a significantly positive return" — same source (literature).
- Kalshi's own calibration paper (Kagan & Baiocchi, 2,243,741 markets, 2021–mid 2026) reports Brier scores falling from about 0.08–0.09 three months out to about 0.02 at close and naive accuracy rising from 88.3% to 97.2%, but publishes no bin-level win rate for 90–99¢ — [Kalshi research summary](https://news.kalshi.com/p/prediction-market-calibration-how-accurate-is-kalshi); [paper page](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/calibration) (literature; the PDF returned HTML / 429 from this machine).
- PredictIt (2016–22, $850 position cap): "much larger pricing biases than previously studied prediction markets", longshot overpricing larger than elsewhere, bid prices that "persistently added to more than 110" — [Zitzewitz, NBER w35845](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845) (literature, abstract only).
- Intrade 2002–07 (Page & Clemen): a price of 0.80 realised 87.4% overall and 92.8% in political markets; bias grows with time to expiration; buying above 0.50 earned 9.55% in out-of-sample tests at a zero discount rate, 2.47% at a 10% discount rate, nothing at 15–25% — [Page & Clemen, Economic Journal 2013](https://people.duke.edu/~clemen/bio/Published%20Papers/45.PredictionMarkets-Page&Clemen-EJ-2013.pdf) (literature).
- Iowa Electronic Markets: "the longshot bias does not show up in IEM prices"; any bias "results from overconfident traders at long horizons" — [Berg & Rietz, IEM working paper](https://iemweb.biz.uiowa.edu/?p=397) (literature).
- Polymarket daily-temperature markets (2,189 city-dates, 21,697 buckets, Dec 2025–Jul 2026): favourite *over*pricing is strongest in the final 1–12 hours and "nearly one order of magnitude larger" than the longshot error (≈ −11 pp realised-minus-price in the 70–90% bin at 6 h); a buy-NO rule on 0.75–0.95 favourites made +50.7% ROI on 184 trades in July but the authors state the results "do not establish executable trading profits" (no depth, spread or slippage) — [arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13593) (literature).
- Measured on this venue, BTC 15-minute up/down, 260 random settled markets 22 Sep–24 Oct 2026, minute-bar `longPrice` from `/v1/price-history?fidelity=1`: side priced ≥0.99 won 25/25 at 300 s before close, 94/94 at 180 s, 174/174 at 120 s, 246/246 at 60 s; side priced 0.95–0.98 won 66/70 (94.3%) at 300 s, 67/72 (93.1%) at 180 s, 37/37 at 120 s, 12/13 at 60 s; one contract at 0.98 with 60 s left lost (`cpc-btc-updown-15m-2026-09-22-1615z`) — [price-history endpoint](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history?symbol=cpc-btc-updown-15m-2026-10-06-1930z&fixedInterval=INTERVAL_ALL&fidelity=60) (measured).
- Measured on this venue, daily high-temperature buckets, 70 random settled events / 420 bucket markets (Apr–Oct 2026), hourly `longPrice` anchored on each market's `endDate` (10:00–12:00Z the next day): 12 h before end, 0.99+ won 50/50 and 0.95–0.98 14/14; 16 h before end, 0.99+ 13/13, 0.95–0.98 14/15, 0.90–0.94 7/8; 20 h before end, 0.95–0.98 only 5/9 and 0.60–0.79 13/28; 6 h before end, 0.99+ 62/65 — the three losers are all three buckets of one event (`temp-mdwhigh-2026-04-29`, Chicago 29 Apr) that each printed 0.99 simultaneously on a book that summed to more than 2.9, so they are stale/erroneous prints, not real quotes (measured).
- Measured on this venue, settled politics/culture/macro events in the cache (31 House primaries, 20 Senate primaries, 17 governor primaries, 26 Emmys, 25 Billboard, 52 Netflix, 9 DWTS, 11 Rotten Tomatoes, 9 CPI, 6 FOMC, 3 NFP, 3 unemployment, Nobel, Brazil round 1 and margin, ≈130 events): the per-market settlement and hourly-ask pull was still running at the gateway's rate limit when this note was written; its output lands in `pm/pol_settled.json` and the summary (win rate of asks ≥0.99, 0.95–0.98, 0.90–0.94 at 0/6/24/72/168 h before the event start) is printed by `pol_settled.py` in the scratchpad (measured, pending — see the addendum at the end of this note if present).

### Inferences
- The literature's favourite edge is real but tiny in dollars: +0.3 to +0.8¢ per dollar at 95–99¢, versus a 1¢ spread (the median on this venue's `ushr` markets is 2¢) and a 0.07–0.33¢ taker fee. A bot paying the ask captures none of it unless the realised rate exceeds the ask by more than the fee, which the Kalshi at-the-quote test says it does not on average.
- Within 60–120 s of a BTC 15-minute close the winner is known to very high confidence (246/246), but the rule-of-three upper bound on the miss rate at n=246 is 1.2%, which is above the 0.93% a 99¢ buyer can afford. More important, Q5 shows that no ask exists at 99¢ in that window.
- Temperature buckets look calibrated at ≥95¢ from about 16 hours before the next-morning end (i.e. once the afternoon high is in), but at 20–24 hours (the morning of the day itself) 95–98¢ prints won only 5/9 — in line with the published finding that weather favourites are overpriced in the hours before the peak. The sample is small and the prices are stored prints, not quotes.
- Politics favourites on this venue cannot yet be evaluated: nothing has settled except a few dozen primaries.

### Gaps
- No PredictIt or Betfair bin-level calibration for 90–99¢ was found in accessible sources; Zitzewitz's full text was not retrievable (NBER PDF returned HTML).
- Kalshi's own paper does not publish high-price bin frequencies in text.
- The gateway's stored `longPrice`/`shortPrice` is a single price series (shortPrice = 1 − longPrice in every sample checked), so historical spreads and depth cannot be reconstructed. Measured on 41 sampled open markets, the latest history point equalled the current best ask in all 9 markets with a spread of 3¢ or more (e.g. `usbm-az-transaccess` bid 0.35 / ask 0.64 / last trade 0.69 / history 0.64) and never equalled the midpoint, so the stored price is the long side's best offer: the win rates above are for taker-executable asks, with the caveat that on an empty bucket book a 0.99 "ask" is a placeholder offer with no information in it (that is what produced the three simultaneous 0.99 Chicago buckets) (measured).

## Q3. Time value: lockup discount on long-dated favourites and whether the venue pays yield on cash or positions

### Takeaway
There is no evidence that Polymarket US pays interest on cash or positions (no help-centre or rules text found; the venue's help site was unreachable from here), while the Rulebook states margin is 100% of the at-risk amount; so any lockup discount embedded in a 90–99¢ price is a cost the favourite buyer bears, not an edge. On the venue's own data a lockup discount cannot be isolated from genuine uncertainty, but the fee-and-spread arithmetic for the election favourites is: buy at 98¢, earn 1.86¢ net if right, 1.9% of cost, which annualises to roughly 40% if settled within about two weeks of 3 Nov and to about 8–9% if held to the listed 15 Jan 2027 expiry, against a single loss costing 52 winners' profit.

### Cited Findings
- Election contract terms: "Margin: Margin is 100% of the at-risk amount"; "Expiration Date will be the earlier of: (i) the day of the release of the qualifying data by the designated Source; or (ii) two years after the expected date"; "Settlement Date and Time will be no later than the day after the Expiration Date"; "Accelerated Settlement: This Contract may be settled on an accelerated basis if a qualifying outcome occurs before the Expiration Date. In the absence of confirmation from a Primary Source, the Exchange ... may accept the consensus of Secondary Sources [Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, AFP, NYT ...]" — [QCX House-election product filing ptc08062614399](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/ptc/ptc08062614399.pdf) (primary).
- Listed market `endDate` for the House winner contracts is 2027-01-15 and for `usho` 2027-02-02, i.e. up to 11–13 weeks after election day (measured).
- Global Polymarket (not the US venue) introduced "holding rewards" of up to 4% annualised in September 2025 as "an interest rate designed to stabilize long-term markets" — [Pyth Network blog](https://www.pyth.network/blog/will-prediction-markets-reach-1-trillion-when-everything-has-a-price) (secondary; global venue only).
- A long-horizon study of Intrade reports biases of 4.7–10.9 pp at prices of 0.20/0.80 for markets with more than 100 days to expiration and models interest-bearing positions as the remedy — [arXiv 2602.21091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21091) (literature).
- Page & Clemen: the Intrade favourite edge disappears at a 15% (strategy A) to 25% (strategy B) discount rate — [Page & Clemen 2013](https://people.duke.edu/~clemen/bio/Published%20Papers/45.PredictionMarkets-Page&Clemen-EJ-2013.pdf) (literature).
- Measured: the House-control market (`usho` Dem 0.905/0.906) and the seat ladder (`hrep-demst` ≥218 at 0.90/0.91) are priced consistently with each other, so no "control vs ladder" lockup wedge exists to harvest (measured).
- Measured ask distribution on the 891 `ushr` nominee markets: 120 asks at 0.99, 144 at 0.97–0.98, 50 at 0.95–0.96, 37 at 0.90–0.94, 39 at 0.80–0.89, 470 below 0.80, 31 unquoted; mean spread in the 0.97–0.98 bucket 2.2¢ (measured).

### Inferences
- Because cash earns nothing on the venue, a 98¢ House favourite held from 25 Oct to a mid-November accelerated settlement is a ≈1.9% gross-of-nothing return on capital over ~2–3 weeks; held to certification it falls to ≈1% per month. A $135 account can hold ~137 contracts, so the whole trade is worth ≈$2.5 per cycle before any loss.
- The tail is the problem, not the yield: one 98¢ loser costs $0.98 against $0.0186 per winner, so the strategy needs better than 98.1% realised accuracy on the specific set of races a bot would pick, with no model to pick them. There is no settled election data on this venue to estimate that, and cross-venue evidence says the realised excess is 0.3–2.6 pp, i.e. the trade is roughly break-even to slightly positive before the 1–2¢ spread.

### Gaps
- No primary source on whether Polymarket US pays interest on uninvested balances; `help.polymarket.us` returned a proxy 502 and `polymarket.us/fees` is a client-rendered page with no readable text.

## Q4. Consistency across related markets on this venue (exclusive sets, House control vs districts, winner vs margin ladders)

### Takeaway
Two-sided nominee pairs are tight (median ask-sum 1.02, bid-sum 0.98 across 427 House races; 6 races had ask-sums below 1.00, the lowest 0.97 on a 4.6k-share bid side) but after the 0.1–0.5¢ fees and the January settlement date the sub-$1 pairs are a 2–3¢ gross, sub-1%-per-month trade; the margin-of-victory ladders are 78% unquoted on at least one side, so no winner-vs-margin arbitrage can even be evaluated; the one class of genuine, fee-positive inconsistency found is stale bids in threshold ladders (e.g. core-CPI ">2.9%" asked at 4¢ while ">3.0%" bid 9¢, 2,700 shares each, ≈4.2¢ net on a 95¢ outlay), which is a few dollars of edge per occurrence.

### Cited Findings
- Measured, 427 `ushr` events with both nominee quotes: sum of best asks median 1.02 (min 0.97, max 1.33), sum of best bids median 0.98 (min 0.77, max 1.01); ask-sum < 1.00 in 6 events (`ushr-wa-02` 0.97, `ushr-co-05` 0.98, `ushr-al-05` 0.99, ...); bid-sum > 1.00 in 7 events (max 1.01) — [gateway listing](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?closed=false&limit=500&offset=0) (measured).
- Depth behind those: `ushr-wa-02` Rep 0.02 × 4,600 bid / 0.04 × 41,000 ask, 14,228 shares traded; `ushr-al-05` Rep 0.93 × 41,000 / 0.94 × 18,000, 7,453 traded, open interest 352; `ushr-ga-07` Rep 0.90 × 36,000 / 0.91 × 8,500, 62,455 traded; `ushr-mi-10` Dem 0.71 × 91,000 / 0.72 × 54,000, 277,315 traded — [bbo endpoint](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/ushrewc-ushr-al-05-2026-11-03-rep/bbo) (measured).
- `usse` (49 events): ask-sum median 1.01 (min 0.95 on a one-market event), bid-sum median 0.99 (max 1.03 on `usse-tx-col`); `usgub` (40 events): ask-sum median 1.01, min 0.94 (`usgub-ny-nas`, a county sub-market), bid-sum max 1.00 (measured).
- House control vs districts: `usho` Dem 0.905/0.906 with 5.4 M shares bid and 5.3 M offered, 65.2 M shares traded, open interest 1.61 M, 4,807 hourly history points since 7 Apr 2026 with 707 price changes; the Democratic seat ladder has ≥218 at 0.90/0.91 (100,018 shares traded), ≥230 0.75/0.76, ≥235 0.54/0.55, ≥240 0.57/0.58, ≥245 0.37/0.38, ≥250 0.19/0.20; summing district mid-prices gives 233.6 expected Democratic seats over 425 quoted districts (226 districts with Dem mid > 0.5, 199 with Rep > 0.5, 41 districts between 0.2 and 0.8, 30 districts unquoted) — [usho bbo](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/paccc-usho-midterms-2026-11-03-dem/bbo) (measured).
- Winner vs margin: of 3,803 `ushrmov` markets only 822 (22%) have a two-sided quote; median spread 17¢, 90th percentile 34¢. Example `ushrmov-ga-03`: quoted Republican-margin bucket asks sum to 1.45 and bids to 0.39 while the Republican winner market is 0.96/0.97, i.e. the no-arbitrage band is 0.39–1.45 around a 0.96 winner. A naive scan flags 125 of 409 districts where quoted bucket asks sum below the winner bid, but in every case inspected the gap is missing legs, not an executable arbitrage (measured).
- Ladder monotonicity: across 77 threshold ladders (`gte`/`gt` families) there were 6 violations where the next-higher threshold's bid exceeded this threshold's ask: `hrep-demst` ≥215 ask 0.88 vs ≥218 bid 0.90 (gross 2¢, fees 1.36¢; depth 2,300 / 5,100 shares); `hrep-demst` ≥235 ask 0.55 vs ≥240 bid 0.57 (gross 2¢, fees 3.42¢ — negative); `usbirth-gfr` 52 vs 52.5 (2¢ vs 2.8¢ fees — negative); `uscpi-october-yoy` >3.8 ask 0.24 vs >3.9 bid 0.23 at listing time (1¢, negative) and >4.2 ask 0.10 vs >4.3 bid 0.14 (gross 4¢, fees 1.46¢; by the re-check the 0.14 bid had gone to 0.01); `uscpicore-october-yoy` >2.9 ask 0.04 × 2,700 vs >3.0 bid 0.09 × 2,700 (gross 5¢, fees 0.84¢, still present on re-check; those two markets have 61 and 13 shares traded in total) — [bbo endpoint](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets/cpic-uscpicore-october-yoy-2026-11-10-gt3pt0pct/bbo) (measured).
- Settled temperature events (70): the sum of the bucket prices 24 h before the market end had median 1.14, min 1.02, max 3.4, i.e. stored prices on thin ladders routinely over-round by 10%+ (measured).
- PredictIt's documented persistent >110 bid-sums were attributed to the $850 position cap — [Zitzewitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845) (literature).

### Inferences
- The two-sided nominee markets are kept within ±2¢ of $1 by someone; the few sub-$1 pairs are worth 2–3¢ gross per $0.97–0.99 locked until settlement (Nov–Jan), roughly 0.5–1% per month before the chance that an ask is pulled between the two IOC legs. A 45-second poller taking one leg at a time carries legging risk on the second leg.
- District aggregation versus the control market is not tradable as an inconsistency: it is model-dependent (seat correlations), the sum of 233.6 + ~30 safe unquoted seats sits plausibly against a ladder median around 236–240, and the account cannot hold 435 legs.
- The only mechanical, fee-positive inconsistency observed is stale resting bids in illiquid threshold ladders. Capacity is set by the stale bid's size (2,200–7,700 shares, i.e. far above the account), but the opportunities are rare (6 in 77 ladders, 2 fee-positive, 1 surviving an hour) and each is worth a few cents per contract; with $1–$25 orders this is single-digit dollars per hit.

### Gaps
- Historical frequency of ladder violations was not measured (would need repeated snapshots; the price-history endpoint stores only one price series per market).

## Q5. Crypto price markets: resolution mechanics, relation to spot, known inefficiencies, and buying the near-certain side in the final minutes

### Takeaway
Polymarket US crypto contracts settle on CF Benchmarks' Bitcoin Real Time Index (BRTI), with the 15-minute up/down taking a 60-observation average over the final minute before each reference time; in 260 settled 15-minute markets the ≥99¢ side won every time from 120 s out, but live top-of-book polling of two windows shows why that is not harvestable: the winner's book becomes one-sided in the final 20–180 s (63,000 shares bid at 99¢ with zero offered; 79,000 shares demanding the other winner with zero sellers), and the last executable ask on the favourite was 98¢ for 13,000–15,000 shares at 30–145 s left, where the measured win rate (93–100%, n=13–72) straddles the 98.1% break-even.

### Cited Findings
- Contract terms: "{continuous-data}: The price of bitcoin, expressed in U.S. dollars, as disseminated by the Primary Source through its Bitcoin Real Time Index feed (BRTI)"; "Primary Source: CF Benchmarks Ltd."; "If the observed value falls exactly on the boundary between two ranges, the Contract will settle in favor of the range for which that value is the inclusive lower boundary"; max/min contracts use "a trimmed mean ... prices ... during the preceding sixty (60) seconds ... top 20% and bottom 20% of values are removed"; "Position Limits: No position limits apply"; "Position Accountability Level ... $25,000 of notional exposure"; data-source-error provision allows a 7-day postponement — [QCX crypto product filing rules09112624948](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09112624948.pdf) (primary).
- 15-minute market description on the venue: "This market will settle to Up if the Bitcoin price at 3:45PM ET on Oct 6, 2026 is greater than or equal to the price at 3:30PM ET ... Outcome sourced from CF Benchmarks' Bitcoin Real-Time Index (BRTI)" — [event](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?slug=btc-updown-15m-2026-10-06-1930z) (measured).
- Press description of the US 15-minute launch: for both reference points "60 BRTI observations are collected during the final minute before the specified time and averaged, with the result rounded to two decimal places" — [The Arabian Post](https://thearabianpost.com/polymarket-adds-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-to-us-app/) (secondary).

- Global-venue 15-minute market snapshots (Nov 2025–Jul 2026): an hour before open "90% of prices sit between 0.495–0.505" — [Substack analysis](https://viktoriatsybko.substack.com/p/does-polymarket-know-which-way-btc) (blog, global venue).
- Measured, 260 settled US 15-minute markets: win rates by favourite price and seconds-to-close as listed in Q2 (0.99: 25/25 at 300 s, 94/94 at 180 s, 174/174 at 120 s, 246/246 at 60 s; 0.95–0.98: 94.3%, 93.1%, 100%, 92.3%); one reversal after the 60-second mark from 0.98 (measured).
- Measured, live top of book of `cpc-btc-updown-15m-2026-10-24-2345z` polled every 4–10 s: 293 s left bid 0.86 × 88,000 / ask 0.87 × 63,000; 178 s 0.91 × 60,000 / 0.92 × 52,000; 120 s 0.97 × 60,000 / 0.98 × 15,000; 87 s 0.90 × 52,000 / 0.91 × 48,000; 58 s 0.92 × 38,000 / 0.93 × 22,000; 41 s and 28 s 0.97 × 39,000 / 0.98 × 13,000; from 23 s to 3 s left bid 0.99 × 63,000 and no ask (askShares 0); this market settled Up. Next window `...-0000z` (Down won): at 297 s left bid 0.08 × 55,000 / ask 0.09 × 81,000; from 177 s left through 3 s left there was no bid at all and an ask of 0.01 × 79,000–100,000 shares (i.e. 79,000–100,000 shares of demand for the winning No side at 99¢ and not one seller for the entire final three minutes) — `pm/live15_log.jsonl` (measured). Polling at 4-second intervals alongside two other scripts produced HTTP 429 on 10 of 118 requests (measured).
- Open-interest scale: the 23:45 window had 17,194 contracts of open interest and 59,871 shares traded with 5 minutes left (measured).

### Inferences
- The structural reason the trade fails is that the BRTI closing reference is a one-minute average, so in the final 60 s the outcome is nearly deterministic for everyone, and the resting liquidity on the winning side is all demand. A taker cannot buy certainty at 99¢; the last offer is 98¢ around 30–145 s out, when the one-minute average has not started and a 0.05% BTC move still flips the result.
- At 98¢ the bot needs ≥98.14% accuracy; measured 0.95–0.98 prints won 93–100% with n ≤ 72 per horizon. Even taking the optimistic 37/37 at 120 s, the expected net is ≈1.9¢ × P(win) − 98¢ × P(loss), which turns negative at a 1.9% miss rate; the observed reversal rate from 0.98 at 60 s was 1/13. There is no measurable edge, and the downside is the whole stake.
- The longer-dated `btc-150k`/`hit-price` ladders are 79% unquoted on at least one side and are ordinary volatility bets with no mechanical mispricing visible.

### Gaps
- Only two live windows were observed; the depth pattern (ask vanishing in the final 20 s) should be re-checked over more windows and at quiet hours before relying on it.
- The gateway's stored price does not record which side was the aggressor, so whether the 0.98 prints were buys at the ask or sells at the bid is unknown.

## Q10. Frank view: which, if any, of these could a 45-second taker bot with $1–$25 IOC orders exploit after the 0.0695·p·(1−p) fee?

### Takeaway
None of the catalogued structures offers a dependable, model-free edge at this fee and order size; the closest are (a) stale bids inside illiquid threshold ladders, which are real but rare and worth single-digit dollars per hit, and (b) a post-release CPI/jobs ladder whipsaw that requires reading the official print. The "buy 97–99¢ near resolution" trade is roughly fair on cross-venue evidence, un-executable in BTC 15-minute markets because the winning side has no sellers in the final minutes, and on election contracts it is a 1–2% return locked for weeks with no settled data on this venue to size the tail.

### Cited Findings
- Fee break-evens (computed from the filed formula): 99¢ needs 99.07% accuracy for a 0.93¢ net gain; 98¢ needs 98.14% for 1.86¢; 95¢ needs 95.33% for 4.67¢; 90¢ needs 90.63% for 9.37¢ — [rules09212628660](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09212628660.pdf) (primary, arithmetic).
- Cross-venue realised frequencies at the top bins are +0.1 to +2.6 pp above price (Q2), and the at-the-quote Kalshi return at 90–95¢ one day out is −0.2% (CI −2.8% to +2.1%) — [calibration results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration/main/reports/results.md) (literature).
- BTC 15-minute final-minute books are one-sided on the winner (Q5) — `pm/live15_log.jsonl` (measured).
- Fee-positive ladder violations found in one snapshot: 2 of 77 ladders, each 2–4¢ net per contract with 2,300–2,700 shares of depth, one gone within the hour (Q4) (measured).
- CPI June 2026: a worthless contract traded at 0.98 two to four minutes after the print (Q6) (measured).
- Sub-$1 nominee pairs: 6 of 427 House races, best 0.97 for a guaranteed $1.00 at settlement (Nov 2026–Jan 2027), fees ≈0.5¢ (Q4) (measured).
- Weather favourites in the hours before the daily peak are documented as *over*priced on the global venue (−11 pp at 6 h in the 70–90% bin) and the only sell-side rule shown profitable there is explicitly "not executable" per its authors — [arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13593) (literature); on this venue 95–98¢ prints 20 h before the end won only 5/9 (measured, tiny sample).

### Inferences
- Ranked by expected value per unit of the user's attention, for a $135 account: (1) ladder stale-bid arbitrage in `hrep`, `senate`, `uscpi`, `usunemp`, `usbirth`-type threshold families — mechanical, fee-positive when gross ≥ ~2.5¢, but requires buying one leg and selling the other (buying the complementary No), two IOC legs with legging risk, and it will produce perhaps a few hits a month; (2) sub-$1 nominee pairs — mechanical but ≈2.5¢ per $0.97 locked for 3–12 weeks, i.e. under 1% a month; (3) election favourites at 97–99¢ — about fair, with a tail that one loss erases; (4) BTC 15-minute "certainty" — not available at a price the fee allows; (5) macro post-release lag — real but needs an external data feed and a sell-side rule; (6) awards, turnout, margin ladders, closest-race — spreads of 15–45¢ make any taker strategy negative from the start.
- A structural point that holds everywhere here: on this venue the No side is the same instrument sold, so "buy the near-certain side" means either lifting a 97–99¢ ask or hitting a 1–3¢ bid; the final-minutes BTC data show that when certainty arrives, the only resting liquidity is on the wrong side for a taker. The same dynamic should be expected on election night and at macro prints: by the time a 45-second poll sees 99¢, the offer is gone.
- Everything measured here is one or two snapshots on a venue three weeks from its first election settlement; the election-night behaviour of these books (halts, accelerated settlement timing, whether 99¢ asks exist after the AP call) is unobserved and is where any real model-free yield would have to come from.
