# Cross-venue price discovery and lag as an edge for a small Polymarket US bot

Scope note: "PMUS" = Polymarket US (polymarket.us, QCX LLC, CFTC DCM). "Global Polymarket" = polymarket.com (USDC/Polygon venue a US person cannot trade). All own measurements below were taken on 24 October 2026 (UTC) from public, unauthenticated endpoints only; raw outputs are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Atlas/8739734e-beef-5b53-b4a5-62b091fed4fa/scratchpad/dl/` (`lag_results.json`, `lag3_out.txt`) and the scripts in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Atlas/8739734e-beef-5b53-b4a5-62b091fed4fa/scratchpad/scripts/` (`lag.py`, `lag3.py`, `kfed.py`, `crypto.py`). Anything dated before 2025 is labelled "pre-2025".

## Where price discovery happens, by contract type (summary of the sections below)

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, PMUS is not the slow venue in any contract type a 45-second poller could exploit: pregame sports it sits within a cent of Kalshi, global Polymarket and de-vigged DraftKings; in play it moves minute-for-minute with Kalshi and *leads* global Polymarket; in politics/macro it tracks global Polymarket and Kalshi within ~1c with dislocations of 3c+ appearing in only ~1–2% of minutes and closing in minutes; in BTC ladders it reprices in the same minute as Coinbase spot.

### Cited Findings
- Sports pregame: across 88 fixtures sampled 13 Oct 2026, the median de-vigged gap to Pinnacle's fair line was 0.59 points for polymarket.com, 0.59 for polymarket.us and 0.75 for Kalshi, with median two-way margins of 1.00%, 0.99% and 1.02% versus Pinnacle 4.02% and bet365 6.70%; polymarket.us's median NFL margin was 0.26% — [OddsPapi developer guide, 13 Oct 2026](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/)
- Sports in play (one September 2026 NFL game): the same team's price on PMUS and global Polymarket was within 3.8c 90% of the time, 15.7c apart at the extreme; facts checked 17 Oct 2026 — [ThinBlimp, PMUS vs Polymarket](https://thinblimp.com/guides/polymarket-us-vs-polymarket)
- Politics/entertainment: Polymarket (global) leads Kalshi in price discovery; sports: Kalshi leads, and leadership follows relative volume and directional large-trade flow (2023–2026 sample, working paper revised 28 Apr 2026) — [Ng, Peng, Tao & Zhou, SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)
- Own measurement, in play, four games on 23 Oct 2026: PMUS and Kalshi 1-minute changes correlate 0.96–0.99 at lag 0, while global Polymarket's changes correlate best with PMUS's *previous* minute (0.36–0.95 at k = −1) — see "Sports in play" below (own measurement; endpoints: [PMUS price-history](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history), [CLOB prices-history](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history), [Kalshi candlesticks](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-market-candlesticks))
- Crypto: PMUS BTC contracts settle on CF Benchmarks' BRTI (published once per second); "Prices on spot exchanges or other indices have no effect on settlement" — [PMUS Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs.md)

### Inferences
- The leading venue differs by contract type (Pinnacle/Kalshi for sports, global Polymarket for politics, spot exchanges for crypto), but in every case PMUS already incorporates the leader's move within roughly a minute, which is faster than the worker's 45-second poll plus REST round trip can reliably beat.

### Gaps
- No academic or dashboard source was found that measures PMUS specifically against other venues; all PMUS-specific numbers here are the OddsPapi/ThinBlimp snapshots and own measurements over 1–14 days.

## Does PMUS share an order book, liquidity or market makers with global Polymarket? How do prices compare and who arbitrages them?

### Takeaway
PMUS and global Polymarket are separate exchanges with separate books, fees, ticks and prices; nothing crosses between them, and no public source identifies who arbitrages them. Measured over 14 days of politics/macro contracts, PMUS stayed within a median 0.2–0.5c of global, with one persistent structural gap (Senate control, PMUS ~2.3c below global) that is not a lag.

### Cited Findings
- "Polymarket US and global Polymarket are separate exchanges with separate accounts, order books, fees, and prices. Trades on one never touch the other." For one September NFL game none of PMUS's 1,076 markets matched a polymarket.com market; tick size 0.1c on Polymarket vs 1c on PMUS (facts checked 17 Oct 2026) — [ThinBlimp](https://thinblimp.com/guides/polymarket-us-vs-polymarket)
- PMUS is a CFTC-registered DCM operated by QCX LLC d/b/a Polymarket US, with NFA providing surveillance under a Regulatory Services Agreement — [integrity.polymarket.us](https://integrity.polymarket.us/)
- PMUS's own gateway metadata lists `orderPriceMinTickSize: 0.001` on NFL moneyline markets, while the repository's captured NBA quotes show `tick 0.010`; observed PMUS spreads at 23:46 UTC 24 Oct 2026 were 0.1c on House control (0.905/0.906), 1c on Fed-October no-change (0.92/0.93), 1c on Georgia Senate (0.94/0.95) — own reads of [gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?slug=usho-midterms-2026-11-03) and `/v1/markets/{slug}/bbo`
- PMUS fee schedule effective 1:00 PM ET 19 Oct 2026: taker fee = 0.0695 × C × p × (1 − p) (0.10 for table tennis); maker rebate only above $10M prior-month maker volume (Θ = −0.0035 to −0.0104); taker rebates start at $250K/month (3%) — [PMUS fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)
- Global Polymarket taker rates by category: crypto 0.070, sports 0.030, finance 0.040, politics 0.040, geopolitics 0; makers pay nothing and receive a rebate share (secondary summary of docs.polymarket.com) — [docs.polymarket.com fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees?r=atso)
- Own measurement, 10–24 Oct 2026, 20,160 one-minute observations per pair, PMUS `longPrice` vs global CLOB 1-minute price, both forward-filled to a common minute grid:
  - Fed October "no change" (PMUS `rdc-usfed-fomc-2026-10-28-nochng` vs global token 1110619…): mean |gap| 1.02c, median 0.5c, p90 1.5c, max 9.0c; PMUS − global mean +0.72c; |gap| ≥ 3c in 333 of 20,159 minutes (1.7%), almost all on 14 Oct 2026 when the contract fell from ~0.80 to ~0.48 and recovered.
  - Fed October "+25bp hike": mean |gap| 0.86c, median 0.5c, p90 1.5c; |gap| ≥ 3c in 1.1% of minutes (max 69.5c is a single stale/bad print).
  - House control (Democrats): mean |gap| 0.93c, max 2.6c, PMUS persistently ~0.9c below global; no 10-minute global move ≥ 2c in 14 days; PMUS price changed 45 times, global 40 times.
  - Senate control (Democrats): PMUS − global mean −2.28c for the whole 14 days (max 4.3c), i.e. a persistent structural discount, not a lag; no moves ≥ 2c on either side.
  - Georgia Senate (Democrats): mean |gap| 0.64c, median 0.2c; PMUS changed price only 11 times in 14 days while global changed 3,448 times.
  - Endpoints: [PMUS](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/price-history?symbol=rdc-usfed-fomc-2026-10-28-nochng&fidelity=1), [global](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history), token ids from [gamma-api](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?slug=fed-decision-in-october-20260617190323537).

- PMUS politics launched 8 April 2026 with $5,000/day in liquidity rewards per eligible politics market split among market makers; PMUS did $255.9M notional in March 2026 — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarket-us-posts-256m-march-launches-politics-built-to-scale/)
- An on-chain study of global Polymarket estimates ~$40M of realized arbitrage profit (intra-platform rebalancing and combinatorial arbitrage) April 2024–April 2025 — [Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer & Suarez-Tangil, arXiv 2508.03474 (AFT 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1); a March 2026 review found 14 of the 20 most profitable global Polymarket wallets are bots — [Finance Magnates, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/prediction-markets-are-turning-into-a-bot-playground/)
- A proposal filed 28 April 2026 would let US users trade on Polymarket's main offshore exchange (separate entity from QCX); PMUS currently offers only fully collateralized contracts — [KuCoin news summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-seeks-cftc-approval-to-reopen-to-us-traders)

### Inferences
- Because no account can hold both books, the only entities that can "arbitrage" PMUS against global are non-US firms with a US-regulated affiliate; the very large, symmetric top-of-book quotes on PMUS House/Senate (5M+ shares each side, 0.1c wide) look like designated market-maker quoting funded by the liquidity-rewards program rather than retail flow, and such a maker will reprice off global/Kalshi within seconds.
- The persistent Senate discount (−2.3c for 14 days) shows the cross-venue gap is not always a lag; it can be a clientele/definition difference that never converges before resolution, and a US person cannot trade the other leg.

### Gaps
- No public dashboard, academic paper or disclosure identifies PMUS's market makers or quantifies PMUS–global arbitrage; the 14-day own measurement covers only five contracts and one high-news day (14 Oct 2026).
- Units of PMUS `bidShares`/`askShares` are not documented; OddsPapi reported PMUS shipping `limit: 0` via a different field, so depth figures should be re-verified before being relied on.

## Kalshi vs PMUS: documented gaps, cross-venue arbitrage, fees, API, same worker on both?

### Takeaway
PMUS and Kalshi are the two venues a US person can trade and they move almost in lockstep: on the October Fed decision the two venues' 1-minute changes correlate 0.56 at lag 0 with a median |gap| of 0.5c, and in-play NBA they correlate 0.96–0.99 with no minute 3c+ apart. Kalshi's fee is marginally higher (0.07 vs 0.0695), its public REST market data needs no key, and its API rate limits (20 read/10 write per second at Basic) are workable for the same worker.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi taker fee = M_taker × 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P); maker = M_maker × 0.0175 × C × P × (1 − P), each rounded up to the next $0.0001; standard schedule is taker multiplier 1, maker multiplier 0 — [Allium documentation of Kalshi's published formula](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series-fee-changes); Kalshi's help page says fees are charged "on the expected earnings on the contract", "some markets have maker fees", and points to the schedule PDF (page dated 19 Apr 2026) — [help.kalshi.com/trading/fees](https://help.kalshi.com/trading/fees)
- As of 10 Oct 2026 the schedule listed 157 series with non-standard fees: 126 charge maker fees, 18 use a 0.5 taker multiplier (mostly baseball), 13 are fee-free (secondary) — [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained)
- Kalshi API rate limits (tokens/second, 10 per request): Basic 200 read / 100 write (~20/10 req/s) on signup; Advanced 300/300 via the upgrade endpoint; higher tiers earned by trailing-30-day volume share (Expert at 0.075% of exchange volume) — [docs.kalshi.com rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)
- Kalshi public REST endpoints need no API key (series, markets, events, `/markets/{ticker}/orderbook`, base `https://external-api.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2`); the orderbook returns bids only — [Kalshi market-data quick start](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/quick_start_market_data); 1-minute candlesticks with yes_bid/yes_ask OHLC via `GET /series/{series}/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks?period_interval=1` — [Kalshi candlesticks reference](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-market-candlesticks); every WebSocket connection requires authentication headers, including the `ticker`/`trade` channels — [Kalshi WebSocket quick start](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/quick_start_websockets)
- Own measurement, unauthenticated Kalshi candles returned HTTP 429 after a handful of 3-day requests in quick succession (24 Oct 2026), so a Kalshi poller should budget for far fewer than the documented authenticated limits when running keyless — scratchpad `kfed.py` log
- 2024 presidential election (pre-2025 data): Polymarket–Kalshi execution-adjusted spreads averaged ~$0.03 and reached $0.07 over sustained intervals (≥30 minutes), with Kalshi on a $0.01 tick and fee-free on that market; across ten platforms and >100,000 events (2018–2025) semantically equivalent markets show persistent execution-aware deviations averaging 2–4% — [Gebele & Matthes, arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)
- Polymarket led Kalshi in 2024 election price discovery; in a 2023–2026 sample Kalshi leads sports and whichever venue has more directional large-trade flow leads — [Ng et al., SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)
- NFL Week 6 (13 Oct 2026) depth at top of book on the thinner side: Kalshi median $13,067, polymarket.com $1,830, Pinnacle 6,000; one fixture had ~$90,776 resting on Kalshi vs ~$3,437 on polymarket.com at the same price — [OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/)
- Kalshi order book at 23:46 UTC 24 Oct 2026, Fed-October "hike 0bps": 157,270 yes @0.92 and 569,619 no @0.06 (i.e. 0.92/0.94 two-way); PMUS same contract 0.92/0.93 — own read of [Kalshi orderbook](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets/KXFEDDECISION-26OCT-H0/orderbook) and PMUS bbo
- Own measurement, PMUS vs Kalshi (yes bid/ask mid from 1-minute candles), 10–24 Oct 2026, ~20,150 minutes each:
  - Fed "no change": PMUS − Kalshi mean +0.55c; |gap| mean 0.85c, median 0.5c, p90 1.5c, max 4.0c; |gap| ≥ 3c in 0.8% of minutes; cross-correlation of 1-minute changes 0.56 at lag 0, 0.16 at k = −1 (Kalshi after PMUS), 0.07 at k = +1; of 30 Kalshi moves ≥ 2c per 10 min, PMUS reached half the move at a median of 0 minutes (8 showed no half-response within 60 min, most being 2c moves inside PMUS's 1c spread).
  - Fed "+25bp": |gap| mean 0.77c, median 0.5c; ≥ 3c in 0.5% of minutes; lag-0 correlation 0.125; median half-response 0 min.
- Posted cross-venue scanners claim 2–5% spreads but give no source and often compare non-identical contracts (Fed "change buckets" vs rate-threshold ladders) — [Apify scanner](https://apify.com/jpmarketdata/prediction-market-arbitrage); [predictmarketcap](https://predictmarketcap.com/arbitrage); one guide notes bank-rail vs Polygon transfers take hours to days and most posted arbs are not arbs after fees — [pm.wiki](https://pm.wiki/fr/learn/prediction-market-arbitrage-after-fees)
- A Michigan court blocked Kalshi sports contracts in Michigan on 1 Sep 2026 pending final judgment; Wisconsin's attorney general sued Kalshi and Polymarket on 23 Apr 2026 over sports contracts — [Squawka](https://www.squawka.com/us/bet/kalshi-vs-polymarket/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-cftc-approval-main-platform-us-report)

### Inferences
- Taking the measured Fed-October distribution at face value, a PMUS/Kalshi two-legged trade needs |gap| > roughly 2 × spread (1c each) + two taker fees (≈0.63c each at 0.92) ≈ 3.3c before it earns anything; that occurred in under 1% of minutes and the gaps closed within minutes, so with a 45-second poll the expected number of actionable hits is a few per month per contract.
- Running the same worker against Kalshi is technically easy (keyless REST reads, documented fee formula, 1-minute candles) and would at least double the set of quotes to compare; it does not create an edge by itself because the two venues already co-move at the minute level.

### Gaps
- Kalshi's official fee-schedule PDF returned HTTP 429 on every attempt, so the per-series multipliers for NBA/NFL/CFB, Fed and House/Senate series are unverified; House/Senate control Kalshi tickers were not located via `series_ticker` guesses, so the Senate structural gap seen vs global was not checked against Kalshi.
- No source documents anyone running PMUS–Kalshi cross-venue arbitrage at scale; whether a single person may hold accounts on both DCMs was not addressed by any source (nothing found prohibiting it).

## Sports pregame: how closely does PMUS track sharp consensus, is "buy below sharp fair" a known pattern, and which feeds are affordable?

### Takeaway
Hours before tip-off PMUS sits within ~0.5–1c of Pinnacle's de-vigged line, of Kalshi, of global Polymarket and of de-vigged DraftKings, which is inside its own 1c spread plus a 0.6–1.7c fee; the consensus-deviation strategy is well documented against *soft bookmakers* (and gets accounts limited), not against low-margin exchanges. Pinnacle via The Odds API costs $30–$249/month at 60-second pre-match refresh; Circa is not on The Odds API.

### Cited Findings
- 88 fixtures, 13 Oct 2026: median de-vigged gap to Pinnacle 0.59 pts (polymarket.us), 0.59 (polymarket.com), 0.75 (Kalshi), 0.83 (bet365); against Pinnacle fair, one Chargers leg showed +0.94% EV on polymarket.com with a $3,592 limit, +5.76% on Kalshi on only $106, +4.51% on polymarket.us with no reported limit — [OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/)
- Own measurement from the repository's captures (DraftKings in `tracking/snapshots.csv`, PMUS in `tracking/polymarket_quotes.csv`; moneyline only, pregame, DK snapshot within 15 minutes of the PMUS quote): 29 away-side quotes on 17 NHL games, 23–24 Oct 2026; |PMUS mid − DK de-vigged fair| mean 0.76c, median 0.36c, p90 1.85c, max 3.4c; DK overround mean 4.26%; only 1 of 29 quotes had the PMUS ask below DK fair by ≥1c after the 0.0695 fee (TOR–EDM, 1.2c, 1.6 h before tip) — local files, script in this message's scratchpad
- Own measurement, 24 hours pregame on 23 Oct 2026 (Bulls–Bucks, Pistons–Heat, Army–Tulsa, Duke–Virginia; 1,440 minutes each): PMUS vs global |gap| mean 0.45–0.91c, max ≤ 2c; PMUS vs Kalshi (NBA only) |gap| mean 0.5–0.7c, max 2.5c; no minute with |gap| ≥ 3c; PMUS changed its price 3–8 times in the 24 hours, global 4–9, Kalshi 3–10 — scratchpad `lag3_out.txt`
- Pre-2025 consensus-deviation evidence: 479,440 games, 32 bookmakers; betting where a book's odds exceeded the consensus-implied fair value returned 3.5% over 56,435 historical bets, 9.9% over 6,994 bets in a 6-month minute-by-minute paper run, and 8.5% over 265 real-money $50 bets ($957.50) before "bookmakers began to severely limit our accounts" a few weeks in — [Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner 2017, arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)
- Pre-2025: 5,478 top-five-league matches over three seasons; only 10 intra-market arbitrage opportunities among eight bookmakers but 1,450 bookmaker-vs-exchange combinations with guaranteed positive return, attributed to different informational efficiency — [Franck, Verbeek & Nüesch, "Inter-market Arbitrage in Sports Betting"](https://ideas.repec.org/p/qut/auncer/2009_61.html)
- Soft books follow Pinnacle slowly: 28 fixtures (28 Aug–1 Sep 2026), 1,234–1,353 Pinnacle moves ≥1%; median lag to follow 6.3 min (1xBet) and 66.8 min (bet365) on the Premier League, vs controls of 53 and 161 min; exchanges and prediction markets were not included — [OddsPapi soft-book lag](https://oddspapi.io/blog/soft-book-lag-pinnacle/)
- CLV practitioner material treats the de-vigged Pinnacle close as the benchmark and says CLV only means much at high volume; no peer-reviewed CLV-to-ROI study surfaced — [SharpAPI CLV docs](https://docs.sharpapi.io/en/api-reference/historical-clv/); [TheSpread](https://www.thespread.com/?p=550721)
- The Odds API plans: Starter free/500 credits, $30/20K, $59/100K, $119/5M, $249/15M credits per month; one request = one credit (× markets × regions); Pinnacle, DraftKings, FanDuel and Betfair covered, Circa not listed; historical snapshots back to 2020 — [the-odds-api.com](https://the-odds-api.com/); refresh: featured markets 60 s pre-match / 40 s in-play, props 60/60 s, outrights 5 min/60 s, exchanges 20/10 s, with the interval sliding toward in-play rate from six hours before start — [update intervals](https://the-odds-api.com/sports-odds-data/update-intervals.html)
- OddsJam: a competitor states it has no public API and sells a $99–$499/month consumer tool — [SharpAPI comparison](https://sharpapi.io/compare/oddsjam-alternative); SportsGameOdds advertises Pinnacle (direct), Circa, Betfair and Matchbook in one feed at $99–$499/month with WebSockets — [SportsGameOdds](https://sportsgameodds.com/bookmakers/circa-odds-api); Circa has no public API and several vendors scrape it — [OddsPapi on Circa](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3108)
- Pinnacle's own API: "permitted for both business partners and individual customers" subject to review of betting history, "variable cost", "Custom latency & Call-Rates", a separate application form for prediction-market traders asking for exchanges, latency needs and monthly turnover, and "Regional API availability will be determined based on each customer's specific use-case" — [pinnacle.com/en/api](https://www.pinnacle.com/en/api); a third-party client notes the API cannot be called from a US IP and requires a funded account — [npm pinnacle-sports-api](https://npmjs.com/package/pinnacle-sports-api)

### Inferences
- The documented "below consensus" edge comes from soft books that misprice by several percent and tolerate it until they limit the account; PMUS, Kalshi and polymarket.com price the game at ~1% margin within 0.6–0.75 points of Pinnacle, so the same signal applied to PMUS has nothing left after a 1c tick and 0.6–1.7c fee. DraftKings (4.3% overround, not sharp) is a weak reference; the repository's DK captures will mostly confirm that PMUS pregame mids sit inside DK's vig.
- The useful purchase, if any, is a Pinnacle-inclusive feed at the $30–$59 tier of The Odds API for *measurement* (60 s refresh is too slow to be a trading signal against a venue that reprices in a minute); Pinnacle direct API access is realistically unavailable to a US retail bettor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Betfair-vs-Pinnacle lead-lag study was found; the only exchange-vs-bookmaker speed evidence is pre-2025 in-play work (next section) and a vendor's soft-book sample.
- The DK-vs-PMUS own check is 29 quotes on one sport over two days; the repository's longer overlap will supersede it.

## Sports in play: who is faster, and how far behind are the existing arbitrageurs from a 45-second poller?

### Takeaway
In play, PMUS and Kalshi move together within the same minute, global Polymarket trails both by about a minute and often more, and the participants who already trade this operate on data feeds that arrive 0.1–0.8 s after the event (courtsiders faster still), so a 45-second poller is one to two orders of magnitude behind and PMUS is not the venue that lags.

### Cited Findings
- Own measurement, in play, 23 Oct 2026 (PMUS `longPrice` vs global CLOB 1-min price vs Kalshi 1-min yes-mid):
  - Bulls–Bucks (150 in-play minutes): PMUS vs Kalshi |gap| mean 0.58c, max 1.5c, lag-0 change correlation 0.994; PMUS vs global |gap| mean 2.63c, p90 8c, max 18.5c, 25% of minutes ≥ 3c; global's 1-minute changes correlate 0.946 with PMUS's previous minute (k = −1) and 0.247 contemporaneously; for 5 global moves ≥ 2c/10 min, PMUS had already made half the move a median 5.5 min earlier.
  - Pistons–Heat (160 min): PMUS vs Kalshi mean 0.59c, max 1.5c, lag-0 0.964; PMUS vs global mean 1.70c, max 12.5c, 15.6% ≥ 3c; global change correlation 0.411 at k = −1; PMUS led by median 6 min on 6 moves.
  - Army–Tulsa (210 min): PMUS vs global mean 2.03c, max 14c, 25.2% ≥ 3c; k = −1 correlation 0.51; PMUS led by median 4 min (8 moves).
  - Duke–Virginia (210 min): PMUS vs global mean 2.15c, max 15.5c, 26.7% ≥ 3c; k = −1 0.362, k = 0 0.372; PMUS led by median 6 min (9 moves).
  - Timestamp check at 23:46:38 UTC: PMUS's latest price-history point was labelled the current minute, Kalshi's latest candle ended at the current minute, and the CLOB's latest point was 62 s old, so up to one minute of the apparent global lag can be sampling convention; multi-minute leads cannot be — scratchpad `lag3_out.txt`
- One September 2026 NFL game in play: PMUS vs global within 3.8c 90% of the time, 15.7c at the extreme — [ThinBlimp](https://thinblimp.com/guides/polymarket-us-vs-polymarket)
- PMUS sports rules apply "to both pregame and in-game trading"; no order delay or suspension policy is published — [PMUS Sports FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/sports-faqs.md); PMUS team records carry both SportsDataIO and Sportradar provider IDs — own read of [gateway events](https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/events?limit=5); Polymarket's August 2026 Genius Sports agreement gives the US product official league data and streaming used for settlement — [SiGMA](https://sigma.world/news/prediction-markets-live-esports-trading/)
- Operators run live streams 5–8 s behind the event (Stats Perform); data feeds arrive in 100–200 ms from elite providers and 300–800 ms from second-tier ones (vendor figures); exchanges and sportsbooks impose in-play delays for different reasons and the length "varies from betting operator to betting operator" — [SBC News, 20 May 2026](https://sbcnews.co.uk/latestnews/2026/05/20/cost-stream-delay-live-betting/); [UK Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/in-play-or-in-running-betting)
- Pre-2025: high-frequency Betfair prices around goals "update swiftly and fully" — [Croxson & Reade, Economic Journal 2014](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/34884); courtsiders on Betfair (141 Grand Slam matches, 2009–2014) speed price discovery, and the early-trading abnormal return is more than 60% of the full price reaction before the public sees the point — [Bizzozero, Flepp & Franck, JEBO 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v156y2018icp126-143.html); an older VAR study of Euro 2008 found "tentative indications that the Betfair exchange leads on price discovery" over two bookmakers — [Exchange vs. Dealers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228720836_Exchange_vs_Dealers_A_High-Frequency_Analysis_of_In-Play_Betting_Prices)
- Polymarket (global) removed a 500 ms taker delay and introduced dynamic taker fees in February 2026; a bot author reports speed "is no longer a standalone edge" and that his surviving bot uses resting maker orders averaging 78 trades/day with EV ≈ $0.03 per trade — [dev.to, self-reported](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)

### Inferences
- Since PMUS and Kalshi are near-identical minute by minute and both run ahead of global Polymarket, reading global in play gives a PMUS bot a *lagging* signal; the only faster reference would be a sub-second scores/odds feed, and the measured in-play PMUS–global gaps of 10–18c show how much the market moves inside a minute, which is the adverse selection a 45-second IOC taker absorbs.
- The in-play dispersion is consistent with the backtest finding of losses from resting bids: resting orders on PMUS are picked off by participants repricing off feeds seconds after each play.

### Gaps
- No published latency figure for PMUS order entry or in-play market-data distribution; whether the PMUS `wss://api.polymarket.us/v1/ws/markets` stream is available without an API key is not stated in the docs (a third-party SDK says both streams require credentials) — [PMUS API introduction](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/introduction.md); [third-party Go client](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/jaracah/polymarket-us-go)

## Politics and macro: does PMUS lag global Polymarket (and Kalshi) after news?

### Takeaway
Over 14 days including one sharp repricing day (14 Oct 2026, Fed-October contract swinging ~30 points), PMUS tracked global Polymarket with a median |gap| of 0.5c; PMUS reached half of each 2c+ global move at a median of 1–7 minutes, sometimes before global, and gaps of 3c+ existed in only 1–2% of minutes; against Kalshi the median half-response was 0 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Own measurement (details and endpoints in the first key-question section): Fed "no change" — 39 global moves ≥ 2c per 10 min; PMUS half-response median 7 min (values from −8 to +58 min; 10 with no half-response within 60 min); an earlier run with a 2-hour window and a 30-minute event spacing gave median 16 min over 31 moves with 5 where PMUS led; cross-correlation of 1-minute changes 0.38 at k = −1, 0.19 at k = 0, 0.19 at k = +1 (symmetric within the ≤1-minute sampling offset). Fed "+25bp" — 37 moves, median half-response 1 min, 15 with none within 1 h (mostly 2c moves at prices near 0.05–0.35 that sit inside PMUS's 1c tick). House, Senate and Georgia Senate had no 10-minute global move ≥ 2c in the window.
- Example of the largest dislocation: 14 Oct 2026 10:26–12:43 UTC the global "no change" price fell from 0.805 to ~0.48 while PMUS went 0.82 → 0.81 → 0.61 → 0.48; the PMUS half-response to the first 2c leg was 48 min, to the next legs 14 and −1 min; PMUS was ahead on the 12:43 leg (−8c in 10 min) — `lag_results.json` events
- PMUS vs Kalshi on the same contract: median half-response 0 min; |gap| ≥ 3c in 0.8% of minutes (see Kalshi section)
- Pre-2025: Polymarket odds "swung from a clear Trump lead to a near tie within hours" after the 10 Sep 2024 debate; Trump-market October 2024 turnover $391M vs $958M naive volume (on-chain) — [Tsang & Yang, arXiv 2603.03136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136); Polymarket led Kalshi in 2024 election price discovery, strongest when liquidity and activity were high — [Ng et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995); 2024 Polymarket–Kalshi execution-adjusted spreads ~$0.03 average, up to $0.07 sustained — [Gebele & Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)
- PMUS trading limits: single-market buy capped at $1,000,000; no minimum order size published — [PMUS trading limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/trading/access-and-limits/trading-limits.md)

### Inferences
- On a macro release the actionable window on PMUS appears to be single-digit minutes and the dislocation a few cents on contracts quoted 1c wide with a 0.6–1.7c taker fee; with a 45-second poll and IOC orders at the ask, a realistic capture is 1–3c on perhaps a handful of events per month, i.e. well under $1 per $25 order per hit.
- Where PMUS differs persistently from global (Senate −2.3c, House −0.9c), it is not converging within the horizon and cannot be hedged by a US person; it is a venue-clientele gap, not a stale price.

### Gaps
- The measurement spans one news-heavy day and four quiet contracts; debate nights or data-release days earlier in 2026 were not sampled because PMUS politics markets only began in April 2026 and the gateway's `fidelity=1` history requires explicit timestamps per call (no "max" interval).
- Which price PMUS's `price-history.longPrice` reports (last trade, mid or ask) is undocumented; the BBO endpoint's `lastPriceSample` suggests last trade per side.

## Crypto: PMUS BTC markets vs spot in the final minutes before resolution

### Takeaway
PMUS's BTC "above X" hourly and daily ladders reprice in the same minute as Coinbase spot (change correlation 0.49–0.73 at lag 0, with a residual 0.2–0.5 at +1 to +2 minutes), settle on CF Benchmarks' BRTI rather than Binance/Coinbase, and carry the same 0.0695 fee that peaks at 1.74c at 50c, so the minute-scale latency edge that bots harvested on global Polymarket's 15-minute markets before February 2026 is not visible on PMUS at 45-second resolution.

### Cited Findings
- PMUS crypto families: Up/Down 15-minute and 60-minute windows, Above/Below and Price Range hourly/daily (5 PM ET)/weekly ladders, One-Touch monthly; settlement = simple average of the 60 BRTI prices in the 60 seconds before the settlement instant; Up/Down open value fixed a few seconds after the window starts; institutional reference data can be cached up to an hour (retail endpoints recommended for fresh prices) — [PMUS Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs.md)
- Own measurement, 24 Oct 2026 20:00–23:49 UTC, Coinbase BTC-USD 1-minute closes ([public candles](https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/BTC-USD/candles?granularity=60)) vs PMUS daily ladder (5 PM ET 25 Oct): strike 80,000 (188 min) correlation of PMUS 1-min changes with spot returns 0.49 at lag 0, 0.25 at +1, 0.19 at +2; strike 80,500 (226 min) 0.56 / 0.28 / 0.20; strike 81,000 0.52 / 0.29 / 0.26; hourly ladder (00:00Z 25 Oct) strike 80,700 (49 min) 0.73 / 0.47 / 0.49, strike 80,800 0.52 / 0.22 / 0.34. Global Polymarket "Bitcoin above $80,000 on October 25" (noon ET, Binance 1-min close; 719 min, only 381 one-minute samples) peaked at +2 minutes (0.27) with 0.10 at lag 0 — scratchpad `crypto.py` output
- Global Polymarket (pre-fee, 2025–Jan 2026): one wallet reportedly turned ~$300 into >$400,000 in a month on 15-minute BTC/ETH/SOL contracts via latency arbitrage against Binance/Coinbase; opportunities "exist only for milliseconds" — [Finance Magnates, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/prediction-markets-are-turning-into-a-bot-playground/); a bot write-up puts the pre-fee lag at 30–90 s on 5-minute markets (23 trades) and notes Binance vs Chainlink divergence of 0.3–0.5% for 15–30 s during fast moves — [Indie Hackers posts](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/latency-arbitrage-in-15-minute-crypto-markets-building-a-polymarket-trading-edge-2026-f77cc226c0); Polymarket began charging fees on 15-minute crypto contracts in January 2026 — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/polymarket-begins-rolling-out-fees-in-us-app-and-crypto-markets); global crypto taker rate 0.070 (highest category) — [docs.polymarket.com fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees?r=atso)

### Inferences
- A 45-second poller cannot catch a 1–2 minute residual adjustment that is itself smaller than the fee plus spread near the money, and the BRTI 60-second average means the last-minute spot print is not even the settlement price; PMUS crypto is a maker's market, not a stale-taker's.

### Gaps
- The own measurement is a four-hour window on one evening; PMUS 15-minute Up/Down markets were not sampled (the gateway search surfaced only ladders), so behaviour in the final minutes of a 15-minute window is untested.

## Legality and terms: may a US person read polymarket.com data while trading polymarket.us, and do either venue's rules restrict latency or cross-venue bots?

### Takeaway
polymarket.com itself serves US visitors a "Continue in view only mode" option and its public CLOB/Gamma endpoints answered unauthenticated reads during this research, so reading is clearly tolerated in practice; the PMUS Rulebook (21 Apr 2026) expressly contemplates automated trading systems and requires throttles, price collars, kill switches and anti-excessive-messaging controls, prohibits spoofing/wash trades/disruptive practices and trading on confidential outcome information, and says nothing against reading other venues' prices or cross-venue arbitrage. The full ToS texts of both sites could not be retrieved (client-rendered pages).

### Cited Findings
- polymarket.com shown to a US visitor: "It looks like you're in the US. Trading is blocked in the United States on polymarket.com. Switch to polymarket.us to trade prediction markets. … Continue in view only mode"; footer: "Polymarket operates globally through separate legal entities. Polymarket US is operated by QCX LLC d/b/a Polymarket US" — own fetch of [polymarket.com/tos](https://polymarket.com/tos)
- 2022 relaunch limited US users to view-only access (pre-2025) — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-relaunches-but-excludes-u-s-traders/); the 2022 CFTC settlement required keeping US persons off the platform; secondary sources say the terms prohibit US persons from creating accounts and ban VPNs — [Allium](https://allium.so/blog/is-polymarket-legal-in-the-us-the-state-of-play/)
- PMUS Rulebook (version 2026.04.21), Rule 5.2(i): "Participants utilizing automated trading systems must implement pre-trade risk controls including order throttles, price collars, and kill switches"; 5.3(b): "Order rate limiter functionality may cap the maximum number of Orders that may be submitted … per second"; 5.3(c): orders or messages "whether manually or via automated functionality, must ensure adequate controls are in place to prevent excessive messaging or other activity that may be deemed detrimental or disruptive"; Chapter 7 (Participant Conduct) bans fraudulent or abusive trading, "disruptive trading practice[s]" including spoofing, overloading the system, delaying others' executions and false depth, collusion to move prices, wash sales, and entering orders "on the basis of confidential information relating to the outcome or likely outcome of the event" — [Polymarket US Rulebook PDF](https://polymarketexchange.com/files/legal/Polymarket%20US%20Rulebook%20(2026.04.21).pdf) (linked from [integrity.polymarket.us](https://integrity.polymarket.us/))
- PMUS Market Integrity policy: prohibits "all types of fraud and market manipulation (including spoofing, wash trading, and fictitious transactions)", attempted manipulation and front-running; three rules on confidential information and positions of influence; real-time surveillance by the control desk with NFA support; sanctions up to termination and referral — [integrity.polymarket.us](https://integrity.polymarket.us/); Polymarket published enhanced integrity rules across both venues on 20 Mar 2026 — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260320997513/en/Polymarket-Publishes-Enhanced-Market-Integrity-Rules-Across-Its-DeFi-Platform-and-CFTC-Regulated-U.S.-Exchange)
- PMUS trading restrictions page covers confidential information, tipped information, positions of influence and contract-specific bans (MNPI holders, players/coaches, event-organizer employees); it does not address automation, APIs, arbitrage or other venues — [PMUS trading restrictions](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/trading/access-and-limits/trading-restrictions.md)
- PMUS API: public market data at `gateway.polymarket.us` with no key (markets, order books, BBO, settlement, price history, events, series, sports, search); trading at `api.polymarket.us` with Ed25519-signed keys; 25 requests/second per source IP shared across endpoints, 429 on breach, orders not processed within 5 s rejected as "Global Rate Limit Exceeded" during latency spikes, higher limits by emailing support@polymarket.us with use case and volumes — [PMUS API introduction](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/introduction.md); [PMUS rate limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rate-limits.md); order types limit/market with GTC, GTD, IOC, FOK — [PMUS orders](https://docs.polymarket.us/concepts/orders.md)
- Global Polymarket publishes an open CLOB API intended for automated trading and market making (secondary description) — [QuickNode guide](https://www.quicknode.com/builders-guide/best/top-10-polymarket-trading-bots); [docs.polymarket.com API](https://docs.polymarket.com/getting-started/api.md)
- The Odds API's redistribution terms and OddsJam/Circa licensing: vendors warn that scraping Circa violates its terms — [OddsPapi on Circa](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3108)

### Inferences
- Nothing found prohibits a PMUS participant from using public prices from polymarket.com, Kalshi or sportsbooks as inputs; the operative constraints are the PMUS automation controls (throttles, kill switch), the 25 req/s IP limit, and the integrity rules, none of which a 45-second IOC poller is near.
- The legal exposure for a US person lies in *trading* on polymarket.com (geoblock/VPN), not in reading it; reading via the public API is what polymarket.com's own banner offers.

### Gaps
- The full text of polymarket.com's Terms of Use and polymarket.us's Terms of Use could not be read (pages render client-side; a text proxy returned 401), so clauses on scraping/automated access and the exact "Restricted Person" definition are unverified; the PMUS Rulebook is cited as the binding exchange document instead.

## Frank estimate: edge after fees and capacity at $1–$25 IOC orders

### Takeaway
Measured dislocations between PMUS and any leading venue are mostly inside PMUS's own tick-plus-fee cost (≈1.6–2.7c round trip at typical prices), appear in ~1–2% of minutes in politics/macro and ~0% pregame, and close within minutes; at $1–$25 per order the expected capture is cents per hit and perhaps tens of dollars per month in the best case, while capacity is not the binding constraint — speed and the absence of a lag are.

### Cited Findings
- PMUS taker fee by price: 0.0695 × p × (1 − p) per contract = 0.63c at 0.10/0.90, 1.46c at 0.30/0.70, 1.74c at 0.50; no maker rebate below $10M/month maker volume; no fee on cancels — [PMUS fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)
- Observed PMUS spreads: 0.1c (House), 1c (Fed, Georgia, NBA) — own BBO reads 24 Oct 2026; PMUS top-of-book resting size 100k–5.4M shares on politics/macro, 1–2M on an NBA moneyline — own BBO reads
- Frequency of |gap| ≥ 3c vs global: 1.7% (Fed no-change), 1.1% (Fed +25bp), 0% (House, Senate, Georgia, four pregame games); vs Kalshi 0.8% and 0.5% (Fed) and 0% (NBA pregame and in play); vs DK de-vigged: 1 of 29 pregame NHL quotes ≥1c after fee — own measurements above
- In play, 16–27% of minutes had |PMUS − global| ≥ 3c, but PMUS led; PMUS–Kalshi never exceeded 1.5c — own measurements above
- 88-fixture snapshot: PMUS prices were "signals, not fillable quotes" because the vendor's feed showed `limit: 0` for polymarket.us — [OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/)
- Polymarket bot author's post-fee economics on global: EV ≈ $0.03 per trade at 78 trades/day with resting maker orders, speed "not sufficient on its own" — [dev.to](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)

### Inferences
- Worked case, Fed-type macro contract near 0.80 with a 1c spread: round-trip cost to take and later exit ≈ 1c spread + 2 × 1.1c fee ≈ 3.2c, so only the ~1–2% of minutes with 3c+ dislocations are candidates, and most of those occur in a single burst day where PMUS catches up within 1–7 minutes; a 45-second poller that reacts on the next poll captures perhaps 1–3c on a $25 order (25–30 contracts at 0.80) → $0.25–$0.90 per hit, a few hits per month per contract.
- Capacity is irrelevant at this size: top-of-book depth on PMUS was 100k shares or more on every politics/macro market read; the strategy would still scale to only a few hundred dollars per month with perfect execution because the dislocations are rare and brief.
- The two things that would change the picture are (a) a sub-second data path (Kalshi/PMUS WebSockets plus a sports data feed), which turns the worker into a feed-latency competitor against firms already there, or (b) persistent structural gaps between PMUS and Kalshi (the only pair a US person can trade both legs of); the measured Fed pair showed none, and the Senate-vs-global discount is unhedgeable.

### Gaps
- All own measurements are 1-minute resolution over 1–14 days on 5 politics/macro, 4 sports and 5 crypto contracts; sub-minute behaviour, other sports, and election-night-style event days are unmeasured, and the repository's coming DK-vs-PMUS overlap (and, if added, a Kalshi capture at the same cadence) is the right way to extend the sample.
