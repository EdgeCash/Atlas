# Atlas — Brand Identity Study

Whether "Atlas" should be the permanent brand. Conducted without assuming the
answer, and with the two owned domains scored against every alternative rather
than given a head start.

**Verdict: YES — keep Atlas, conditional on one legal opinion.** Runner-up:
**Lodestar**. The reasoning is in `FINAL_5_BRANDS.md`; the evidence is here.

> **Updated 23 September 2026.** The trademark search this study named as its
> one outstanding pre-launch item has since been run. It found a live US
> federal registration of the identical bare word **ATLAS** in the identical
> class — Reg. 7,995,348, class 041, owned by Atlas FC's holding company —
> whose recital is narrowed exhaustively to **soccer**. The YES below stands
> only if a trademark attorney reads that narrowing as leaving American
> football information registrable. See `TRADEMARK_SEARCH.md`.

## Two findings that decided it

Both are checkable in this repository, which is why they outrank taste.

### 1. The product's voice needs a name that can act

`BRAND_GUIDE.md` defines **permitted verbs for what Atlas does**: *projects ·
reads · measures · grades · moved · sits · agrees · disagrees*. The name is the
grammatical subject of those verbs, and it appears that way **76+ times** in
shipped copy:

```
29  "Atlas has …"          10  "Atlas projects …"     5  "Atlas grades …"
14  "Atlas and the market" 7   "Atlas publishes …"    3  "Atlas marks …"
```

On the card: *"Atlas sits 11.2 points from the market."* *"Atlas marks its own
card down."* The second is arguably the best sentence in the product.

**This is a hard filter most candidate names fail.** "Sightline projects 52.3"
is wrong — a sightline is a thing, not an agent. "Edge Equation projects 52.3"
is worse. A name that cannot take those verbs does not cost a rename; it costs
a rewrite of the voice, and the voice is what the product is.

Atlas passes because it reads as a proper noun with agency — a desk, an
institution, a figure. Of 100 candidates, **31 pass this test**.

### 2. "Edge Equation" is forbidden by the product's own brand guide

`BRAND_GUIDE.md`, language rules:

> Also forbidden anywhere in the product surface: pick, selection, **edge** as
> a noun meaning opportunity, ROI, bankroll, Kelly, stake, parlay…

And the naming table:

> | Model minus market | **difference** | *never* "edge", "value" |

"Edge Equation" is precisely the prohibited usage, in the company name. The
product would be unable to say its own name on a page that obeys its own
rules. (The word is not in the automated `FORBIDDEN` tuples — it has legitimate
uses like "edge case" — so this is a documented prohibition rather than a
failing test. It is no less binding.)

"Edge" is also the single most gambling-coded word in the English language
after "bet". Every tout account in the category sells an edge. A product whose
entire position is *we are not that* cannot be called that.

**Edge Equation is eliminated on category grounds, not preference.**

## Phase 1 — brand landscape

Eleven names, what each does, and what it teaches.

| Brand | Why the name works | Emotion | Lesson for Atlas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Athletic** | Definite article + adjective-as-noun. Claims a whole category by naming the quality, not the thing. | belonging, seriousness, membership | The definite article is the cheapest premium signal available. |
| **ESPN** | An acronym whose expansion nobody remembers. Pure accumulated equity. | default, ubiquity, neutrality | Initialisms only work with decades behind them. Never start with one. |
| **PFF** | Started as *Pro Football Focus* — descriptive, earned trust, then compressed. | rigour, insider credibility | Descriptive → initialism is a **path**, not a starting point. |
| **Baseball Savant** | Category + a human quality. "Savant" signals depth without arrogance. | reverence, deep expertise | A warm human noun beats a technical one for an analytics product. |
| **FiveThirtyEight** | The number of US electoral votes. Meaningless until explained, then unforgettable. | quantitative authority | A name that needs explaining can work *if* explaining it is itself interesting. |
| **Bloomberg** | A surname. Authority by association with a person who stood behind it. | institutional, expensive, definitive | Surnames buy accountability. Atlas's equivalent is the reliability record. |
| **Morningstar** | Warm, celestial, non-financial — for a ratings company. Deliberately soft. | trustworthy, independent, calm | **The closest analogue to Atlas.** A *ratings* business with a gentle, non-technical name. It works because the softness offsets the severity of what the product does. |
| **StatMuse** | Stat + muse. Playful, consumer, answer-machine. | fun, quick, casual | Compounds read as consumer products, not institutions. |
| **Rotowire** | Insider jargon ("roto") + a newswire. | fast, insider, fantasy | Jargon in a name locks you to the audience that knows the jargon. |
| **Action Network** | "Action" is a gambling euphemism for money in play. | transactional, urgent | This is precisely the naming space Atlas must not enter. |
| **BettorSheets** | Literally "bettor" + "sheets". | utilitarian, functional | Naming the *user* fixes the product to that user forever. |

### The two that matter

**Morningstar** is the strongest precedent. It grades financial products,
publishes its methodology, and is named something warm and celestial rather
than *RatingsCorp*. The softness is what makes the severity credible — a harsh
name plus harsh judgements reads as a scold; a calm name plus harsh judgements
reads as a standard.

Atlas occupies the same position: a calm, classical name attached to a product
that will tell you its own numbers are worthless 19% of the time.

**Action Network** is the anti-pattern, and it is worth being precise about
why. "Action" is not a gambling word by accident — it is the euphemism. Any
candidate name sharing that register is disqualified regardless of how it
scores elsewhere. That rule eliminated 14 of the 100 candidates.

## Phase 6 — the domain question, answered structurally

`DOMAIN_STRATEGY.md` has the detail. The strategic finding belongs here:

**`.football` is the correct TLD for this product, not a compromise.**

The scope is fixed — NCAAF now, NFL staged, nothing else
(`ATLAS_FEATURE_ROADMAP.md`: *"No other sports"*). A `.football` domain is
exact, memorable, unambiguous in a social post, and it covers the entire
declared universe. It also releases the study from the single biggest
constraint on naming: `.com` availability.

That is why single dictionary words are on the table at all. `atlas.com` is
unobtainable; `atlas.football` is not.

The cost is that `.football` reads as newer and slightly less institutional
than `.com`, and some readers will mistype `.com`. Both are acceptable for a
launch-stage product whose acquisition is social rather than typed.

### The two defensibility problems, stated plainly

**Crowding in software.** Atlassian, MongoDB Atlas, Atlas Obscura, Atlas Air,
Atlas Copco, Atlas Van Lines. "Atlas" is close to the most-used name in
technology. Trademark is class-specific and sports information is a different
class, but the *search* consequence is real.

**A direct sports collision.** **Club Atlas** is a Liga MX football club,
founded 1916. For a product on a `.football` domain, that is the sharpest
conflict in the study — sharper than any tech overlap. *(This was written as an
estimate. The trademark search since confirmed it is worse than estimated: the
club's holding company holds a live US registration of the bare word in class
041. See `TRADEMARK_SEARCH.md`.)*

### Why they do not disqualify it

**Because this product does not acquire through brand search.** `LAUNCH_PLAN.md`
already concluded Atlas will grow through social and word of mouth, and that
search will arrive on *matchup* queries — "utah iowa state prediction" — not on
the brand name. The crowding tax is levied on a channel Atlas has already
decided not to fight for.

**Because Club Atlas is soccer, in Spanish, in Mexico.** The audience overlap
with American college football cards is close to zero, and no reasonable person
confuses them in context. *(The register partly agrees: every clause of Reg.
7,995,348 carries a `namely` limitation to soccer. Whether that narrowing is
enough to clear American football information is a lawyer's call, not this
study's.)*

**Because the alternative costs the voice.** The 76 sentences above are not
decoration. They are the product's register, they are enforced by tests, and
they are the reason the card reads like a research desk rather than a
dashboard.

## What would flip this to NO

Stated in advance so the decision is falsifiable rather than defended:

1. **A trademark opposition** in class 9 or 41 that a search turns up.
   **Run since — see `TRADEMARK_SEARCH.md`.** It found not an opposition but a
   prior registration, which is the thing that causes them: the identical bare
   word, class 041, live, held by Atlas FC's holding company, with a recital
   limited throughout to soccer. This condition is now **partly met**, and it
   is met in the only way this study said could overturn the YES.
2. **Expansion beyond football.** `.football` becomes wrong, `.com` becomes
   necessary, and `atlas.com` is unobtainable. A bare "Atlas" without a
   category TLD is much weaker.
3. **Paid acquisition becoming the plan.** Bidding on a term that crowded is
   expensive, and the defensibility score would start costing money.
4. **A serious investor or partner objecting** on distinctiveness grounds. It
   is the one criticism of the name that is fair.

**The first now partly applies.** Conditions 2 through 4 do not. The window to
change still costs 176 files and one afternoon — which is why the one open
question is worth a lawyer's hour now rather than after launch, when the cost
becomes permanent.

## The switching cost, measured

| | |
|---|---|
| Files containing the name | **176** |
| Occurrences in code and tests | ~1,004 |
| Occurrences in documentation | ~785 |
| In CSS and JS | 2 |

A mechanical rename plus a domain. **This is the last moment it is cheap** —
after launch it costs the social cards already in circulation, the inbound
links, and whatever recognition exists.

That cuts both ways, and it is why the study was worth doing: the cost of
changing is near zero today and permanent tomorrow, so *now* is when the
question deserves a real answer rather than a shrug.

| | Atlas | Edge Equation | Forge Sports Analytics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency gate | **pass** | fail | fail |
| Category gate | **pass** | **FAIL** — "edge" | pass |
| Memorability | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Trust | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| Premium | 5 | 2 | 2 |
| Expandability ×2 | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| Social | 5 | 2 | 1 |
| Domain | 3 | 5 (owned) | 5 (owned) |
| Defensibility ×2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| **Weighted** | **41** | **26** | **21** |

**Edge Equation** — eliminated by the category gate. Beyond the prohibition:
it is two words, eleven syllables of mouth-feel, reads as a quant fund, and
cannot act as a subject.

**Forge Sports Analytics** — passes the gates, fails everything else. "Forge"
means to make *and* to counterfeit; for a product whose claim is verifiability,
"a forgery" is an unfortunate second reading. "Sports Analytics" as a suffix is
a 2015 name and dates the company on day one. Three words is too long for a
wordmark next to a grade, and "Forge Sports Analytics projects 52.3" is not a
sentence anybody writes.

**Owning a domain is not an argument for a name.** Both were scored as if
unowned, exactly as instructed, and both lose to candidates with no domain at
all.

## The final question

> If Atlas launched tomorrow and could never change its name again, would Atlas
> be the recommended choice?

# YES — subject to one legal opinion

One winner: **Atlas**. One runner-up: **Lodestar**. The full case for both, and
the conditions under which Lodestar takes over, are in `FINAL_5_BRANDS.md`.

The study's own reasoning is unchanged: on voice, fit, expandability and
switching cost, Atlas wins. What changed is that the pre-launch check this
study asked for has been run and came back with a live identical registration
in the operative class. **Get a clearance opinion on US Reg. 7,995,348 before
launch. If counsel says the soccer limitation does not clear American football
information, the answer becomes NO and the name becomes Lodestar.**
