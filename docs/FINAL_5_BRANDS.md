# Atlas — The Final Five, and the Decision

One winner. One runner-up. One explanation.

---

## The five

| | Name | Weighted | The single strongest thing | The single weakest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Atlas** | 41 | the voice is already built on it | defensibility — crowded, plus Club Atlas |
| 2 | **Lodestar** | 40 | everything Atlas does, twice as defensible | one syllable longer, and it implies following |
| 3 | **Brier** | 42 | the most defensible and the most *meaningful* name in the study | nobody outside forecasting knows it |
| 4 | **Almanac** | 38 | the warmest, and the only other one a fan finds comfortable | weak agency in a headline |
| 5 | **Calibre** | 36 | calibration + "a player of that calibre" | two spellings, gun-bore register |

---

## Why the highest score does not win

**Brier scores 42 and comes third.** That is the study's one deliberate
divergence between the ranking and the recommendation, and it is recorded
rather than concealed by re-weighting.

The Brier score is the canonical measure of forecast calibration, in use since
1950. Atlas's grade *is* a calibration measure. The name is a near-perfect
encoding of the product's differentiator, it is essentially unclaimed, and it
is short and spellable.

It loses on the thing this product cannot afford to lose on. From
`FIRST_TIME_USER_AUDIT.md`:

> The brief says *"a user arrives from X."* That user does not land on the
> homepage. They land on **a card**, because every social post links to a
> specific game.

A first-time visitor arriving on a card sees the wordmark for about one second
and will never read the "why Brier?" page. A name whose meaning is locked
behind an explanation is a name that has given up on that second.

Atlas and Almanac are the only two finalists a sports fan understands without
being taught, and Almanac fails the headline test.

---

## The winner: **ATLAS**

### 1. The voice is already built on it, and the voice is the product

`BRAND_GUIDE.md` defines permitted verbs — *projects, reads, measures, grades,
moved, sits, agrees, disagrees* — and the name is their subject **76+ times**
in shipped copy. On every card:

> *Atlas sits 11.2 points from the market.*
> *Atlas marks its own card down.*

That second sentence is the product's whole position in six words. It works
because "Atlas" reads as an institution that can be held to account — a desk,
a body, an agent. Three of the five finalists cannot take that sentence at all.

This is not switching cost. A rename is 176 files and an afternoon. It is that
the sentence would have to be **rewritten**, and it is the best sentence
anybody wrote in this entire project.

### 2. The metaphor is exactly right, and quietly so

An atlas is a **reference you consult, one page per place**. Atlas is a
reference you consult, one card per game. It is comprehensive, it is
consulted rather than followed, and it does not tell you where to go.

That last clause matters more than it looks. The product's defining discipline
is that it never names a side. A compass points. A lodestar is followed. A
bellwether leads. **An atlas just shows you what is there** — which is the only
metaphor in the final five that is consistent with the one thing the product
refuses to do.

### 3. It passes every avoidance test cleanly

Gambling, sportsbook, sharp picks, tout, Discord, influencer — Atlas carries
none of that register. `PRODUCT_VISION.md` positions the product as *The
Athletic + Apple + Bloomberg*, and Atlas sits naturally in that company.

### 4. Morningstar is the precedent, and it is close

A ratings business, publishing its methodology, named something warm and
celestial rather than *RatingsCorp*. The softness is what makes the severity
credible: a harsh name delivering harsh judgements reads as a scold; a calm
name delivering them reads as a standard.

Atlas will tell a reader 19% of the time that its own numbers are not worth
leaning on. It needs the calm name.

### 5. The defensibility penalty is levied on a channel Atlas has already left

The real weakness is crowding — Atlassian, MongoDB Atlas, Atlas Obscura — plus
one sharp sports collision in **Club Atlas**, a Liga MX club, which stings on a
`.football` domain.

But `LAUNCH_PLAN.md` already concluded:

> **Not by ranking for "college football picks"** … Atlas has three plausible
> discovery routes … Social and word of mouth first, search second.

and that search arrives on *matchup* queries — "utah iowa state prediction" —
not on the brand name. **The crowding tax is charged on brand search, which is
a channel this product decided not to fight for eighteen months before it
launched.** Club Atlas is soccer, in Spanish, in Mexico; the audience overlap
with American college football cards rounds to zero.

A weakness on a criterion you have already opted out of is a weakness you can
carry.

---

## The runner-up: **LODESTAR**

The best name in the study that is not the incumbent, and the one to take if
any of the flip conditions fires.

**What it does better.** A defensibility score of 4 against Atlas's 2. No
sports-category collision. Same navigational family, so nothing about the
design system, the map metaphor or the voice would have to change — the
graticule corner becomes a four-point star and the rest of the identity holds.
`lodestar.football` is almost certainly clean.

**What it does worse.** Eight letters against five, which costs a little beside
a 92px grade mark. Slightly literary where Atlas is plain. And the meaning has
one wrong note: **a lodestar is something you follow.** Atlas is emphatic that
it will not be followed — it publishes information and readers make their own
decisions. That is a small semantic friction, and it is the entire reason
Lodestar is second rather than first.

---

## What would flip the decision

Written in advance so this is a falsifiable recommendation, not a defended one.

| Condition | Then |
|---|---|
| A trademark search finds an opposition in class 9 or 41 | **Lodestar** |
| The product expands beyond football, so `.football` stops working and a bare `.com` is needed | **Lodestar** — `atlas.com` is unobtainable |
| Paid search becomes the acquisition plan | **Lodestar** — bidding on a term that crowded is expensive |
| An investor or partner objects on distinctiveness | reconsider; it is the one fair criticism |

**The trademark search has not been done and should be, before launch.** It is
a lawyer's task, not this study's, and it is the single item that could
overturn a YES.

---

## The final question

> If Atlas launched tomorrow and could never change its name again, would Atlas
> be the recommended choice?

# YES

---

## In one paragraph

Atlas is not the most defensible name available, and the study says so plainly
— Brier is, by a distance. It is the name that best fits *this* product: a
reference you consult rather than a signal you follow, calm enough to carry a
harsh judgement, short enough to sit beside a failing grade, and already the
grammatical subject of the best sentence anybody wrote here. Its weakness is
brand search, which this product decided not to compete in. **Keep Atlas.
Register the domains listed in `DOMAIN_STRATEGY.md`, run the trademark search
before launch, and hold Lodestar in reserve.**
