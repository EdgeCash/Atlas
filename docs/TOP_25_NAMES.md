## Notes on the scoring

**Brier scores highest (42) and does not win.** It is the most defensible name
in the study — the Brier score is the canonical measure of forecast
calibration, which is exactly what the grade computes — and it is nearly
unclaimed. It loses on the criterion the table under-weights: a sports fan
arriving from X has no idea what it means, and *"Brier"* teaches them nothing.
`FIRST_TIME_USER_AUDIT.md` established that most first visits are a card from a
link, with no context. A name that requires an explanation the visitor will
never read is a name that is working against the twenty-second test.

This is the study's one place where the numeric ranking and the recommendation
diverge, and it is deliberate. It is recorded here rather than hidden by
adjusting the weights after the fact.

**Assay (38)** has the same shape: superb meaning — to test the purity of a
metal — and too rare to carry a consumer product.

**Fathom (32)** is the best *word* in the list: measure depth, and understand.
It dies on availability. Fathom Analytics is an established analytics company
and Fathom.video is well funded. Directly adjacent category, twice.

**Meridian, Sentinel, Vantage, Keystone, Beacon** all score 4s across the top
and 1–2 on defensibility. They are what a naming agency produces: pleasant,
premium and already in use by eleven companies each.

**Almanac (38)** was the surprise. A sporting almanac is a real and beloved
category, it is warm rather than technical, it takes the definite article well
("The Almanac"), and it says *a reference you consult every season* — which is
the retention story from `RETENTION_DRIVERS.md`. It is the only name besides
Atlas that a sports fan would find immediately comfortable.
