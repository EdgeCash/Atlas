# Atlas — Trademark Knockout Search, Classes 9 and 41

Run 23 September 2026 against the US federal register via public mirrors.

> ## This is not legal advice
>
> I am not a lawyer and this is not a clearance opinion. It is a **knockout
> search**: it looks for obvious blocking marks and reports what it found. It
> does **not** cover common-law rights, state registrations, phonetic
> equivalents, foreign registers, or the likelihood-of-confusion analysis a
> professional would apply. **The finding below needs a trademark attorney's
> opinion before launch.**

**registered United States trademark**, and it is worse than estimated: not a
foreign club with a similar name, but the identical word registered in the
exact class this product would file in.

## What saves it, and what does not

**The recital is narrowed exhaustively to soccer.** Every clause carries a
`namely` limitation. In full:

> Provision of information relating to **soccer**; … entertainment services in
> the nature of **soccer games**; … providing news and information in the field
> of sports, **namely, in the field of soccer**; … providing information
> relating to sports and sporting events, **namely, soccer and soccer events**;
> … *[every remaining clause likewise limited to soccer]*

That narrowing is almost certainly the product of an examiner's requirement,
and it is the single most important fact in this search.

**A caution about how this was nearly misread.** A search-result summary
returned the phrase *"providing news and information in the field of sports"*
and stopped there — which reads as a blocking registration over all sports
information. The full recital continues `namely, in the field of soccer`. The
snippet was not wrong; it was truncated before the clause that limits it.
**Anyone repeating this search from summaries rather than full recitals will
reach the wrong conclusion.**

### The risk that remains

Atlas Sports Intelligence would file in class 41 for something like *providing
online non-downloadable information in the field of American football*. Against
Reg. 7,995,348 that is:

| Factor | Reading |
|---|---|
| Similarity of marks | **identical** — same word, standard character |
| Class | **identical** — 041 |
| Services | **different sports**, same activity (providing sports information) |
| Channels of trade | **overlapping** — both are websites for sports fans |
| Consumers | **overlapping** — sports fans |

Identical mark plus related services in the same class is the classic setup for
a §2(d) refusal. "Soccer" and "American football" are genuinely different
sports with different audiences — especially in the United States, where the
two words denote different games — and an examiner may well accept the
distinction. **That is a judgement call, and it is the judgement a lawyer is
for.**

A second exposure sits behind examination: Kondaira/Prodi now holds a US
registration it has an interest in policing, and opposition is available even
where examination passes.

**SMT-ATLAS** is the closest class 9 mark — a sports technology company, in

**OpenAI's ChatGPT Atlas** matters less as a conflict than as a signal: a very
well-resourced applicant has put an ATLAS-formative mark into classes 9, 35, 38
and 42. It will dominate search results for "Atlas" plus software for years.

### The class 9 question the brief did not ask

**Atlas has no downloadable software.** It is a static site — 182 HTML pages
and 40 lines of JavaScript, no app, no SaaS login. Class 9 covers *downloadable*
software and does not obviously apply.

The classes that actually matter for this product are:

| Class | Applies? |
|---|---|
| **41** | **yes — primary.** Providing online non-downloadable sports information. This is where the conflict is. |
| 42 | only if a hosted tool ships. Crowded but with unrelated services. |
| 9 | **only if an app ships.** Crowded. Currently moot. |
| 35 | not applicable — no advertising or retail. |

So the search was asked about 9 and 41, and the answer is that **41 is where
the problem is and 9 is mostly hypothetical.**

## Coverage — what this search did not do

Stated plainly, because a knockout search that oversells itself is worse than
none.

- **The register was not enumerated.** USPTO's TSDR and bulk APIs now require
  an API key, the new Trademark Search system is behind
  JavaScript, and WIPO's Global Brand Database is behind a challenge. Findings
  came from targeted lookups of marks that surfaced through search, verified
  against full register records where reachable.
- **No common-law or state search.** Unregistered use can block a federal
  application and is invisible here.
- **No phonetic or near-miss search** — ATLIS, ATLAAS, ADLAS and similar.
- **No design-mark search.**
- **Foreign registers not searched** beyond what surfaced. Relevant if the
  product ever ships outside the US.
- **No file-wrapper review.** Office actions, disclaimers, consents and
  co-existence agreements on Reg. 7,995,348 were not read, and they could
  change the picture in either direction.

## What this does to the recommendation

`FINAL_5_BRANDS.md` set out flip conditions in advance. The first was:

> | A trademark search finds an opposition in class 9 or 41 | **Lodestar** |

What was found is not an opposition — it is a **prior registration**, which is
the thing that causes oppositions. It is squarely within the spirit of the
condition and it is the strongest version of it.

**The recommendation is now conditional rather than clean:**

> **Keep Atlas if — and only if — a trademark attorney reviews US Reg.
> 7,995,348 and concludes the soccer limitation makes American football
> information registrable. If counsel says the risk is material, switch to
> Lodestar before launch.**

I am not going to resolve that question by guessing. The soccer narrowing is a
real and substantial argument, the services are genuinely distinct sports, and
the analysis is exactly the kind of judgement a professional makes and I should
not simulate.

### Why this is worth paying for now

The switching cost is **176 files and one afternoon** today, and permanent
after launch. A clearance opinion on one registration is a small, bounded piece
of work, and it is the last item standing between the brand decision and a
launch that cannot be taken back.

### If it goes the wrong way

Lodestar is ready. `FINAL_5_BRANDS.md` has the full case; this search adds the
evidence that its class 41 space is empty. The identity work is the same

design system, the voice or the metaphor has to change.
